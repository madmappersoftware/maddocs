Audio Input & Beat Detection

MadMapper’s advanced audio processing engine allows you to synchronize your visual mappings and DMX lighting with live sound environments. Audio-reactive systems use beat detection and frequency analysis to drive parameters in real time. Using sophisticated algorithms to analyze the audio spectrum, you can transform simple video loops and static lights into dynamic, organic visual performances.

 

There are a number of ways to work with real-time audio inputs in MadMapper.

 

The Conductor / Timelines

If you drag an audio clip into the conductor, MadMapper will automatically analyize the audio clip and detect a BPM.

Audio Configuration & Setup

Before mapping parameters to sound, you must route your audio signal into MadMapper’s engine.

  • Select Your Interface: Navigate to Preferences > Audio. Here, you can select your desired physical or virtual audio interface.

  • Assign Analysis Channels: Within the Audio Preferences, designate the specific "Audio Analysis Channels" you want the software to listen to. These channels act as the source for beat detection, general waveform generation, and spectrum analysis used across your project.

Global BPM & Beat Synchronization

To keep your project perfectly timed to the music, MadMapper relies on a Global BPM clock located in the Master Settings tab. This tempo can be established using several methods:

  • Audio Beat Detection: Analyzes the live incoming audio signal to automatically calculate and adjust the tempo in real time.

  • Ableton Live Link: Synchronizes MadMapper perfectly over a local network with Ableton Live or any other Link-enabled software.

  • MIDI Clock: Receives tempo data directly from external DJ hardware, drum machines, or controllers.

  • Manual Input: Allows you to manually type the BPM or use a "Tap Tempo" function to dial in the speed.

Once a solid BPM is established, you can use it to automate elements of your show. For example, if you are using the Cue Scheduler or Cue auto-play settings, you can instruct MadMapper to automatically switch to the next cue after a specific duration of beats (e.g., triggering a new look exactly every 4 or 8 beats).

 

With the Conductor > Configure Timeline Settings inspector, you can set the playback of your timeline to follow the Default speed, A set speed, the Global set BPM, or External Sync (such as Ableton live, LTC, MTC, or ArtNet).

 

Audio Analysis & Reactive Parameters

Instead of just syncing to the beat, MadMapper allows for highly granular, audio-reactive control by dividing the incoming audio into distinct frequency bands (typically Low, Mid, High, plus overall response).

  • Assigning an Audio Control: The easiest and most direct way to make a parameter react to music is to right-click on virtually any slider or widget in the MadMapper interface (such as Brightness, Contrast, or a DMX fixture's intensity) and navigate to Add Control > Audio. From the resulting pop-out menu, you can instantly assign specific audio drivers:

 

 

  • General Levels: amplitude
  • Frequency Bands: bass, medium, treble
  • Tempo & Timing: bpm, beatCount, or specific divisions like 4_beats, 8_beats, etc.

 

Refining the Reaction: Once assigned, this allows you to bind a heavy kick drum (bass) to the scale of a geometric shape, while a high-hat (treble) triggers a strobe effect on a specific LED wash.

Advanced Audio Control Settings: By opening the Control List and navigating to the Audio tab, you unlock deeper customization for how the mapped parameter responds to the audio signal:

    • Source and Target Ranges: Map the incoming audio level (Source) to a constrained or inverted parameter output (Target). For example, you can translate a standard 0 to 1 audio spike into a broader -1 to 1 visual oscillation range.

    • Filters: Apply mathematical algorithms to manage the incoming audio signal over a specified duration (in seconds). Options include Average, Weighted Average, Pulse Detection, Attack Release, and Damper. These tools allow you to prevent visual flickering, smooth out erratic volume spikes, or dial in precise peak responsiveness.

    • Curves: Toggle response curves to dictate exactly how the visual parameter scales against the audio volume. Available interpolation methods include None, Linear, Hermite, and Catmull Rom. Selecting a curve opens an interactive graph where you can add points and sculpt the exact reactivity profile, giving you ultimate control over the behavior at different volume thresholds.
