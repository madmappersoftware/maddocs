Warping & Geometry

In projection mapping, the physical world is rarely perfectly flat or rectangular. MadMapper provides a powerful suite of warping and geometry tools to deform your digital video canvas so it perfectly wraps around physical architecture, curved objects, and complex stage designs.

 

 

Setup & Preparation: The Grid Generator

Before manipulating complex geometry, it is highly recommended to project a visual test pattern. You can add a Grid Generator media on top of your image and adjust the columns and rows to correspond to the architectural features of the building or object you are mapping.

 

Tip: In the left panel, change the Grid Generator's opacity to 50% and the Blend Mode to Additive. This allows you to clearly see the physical background behind the grid, making the warping process much easier.

 

Output Perspective & Corner Pinning

Setup the output parameters of your surface, including the activation or de-activation of Perspective pinning:

By default, moving the four corners of a Quad surface performs a perspective distortion (often called Corner Pinning). Make sure the "Perspective" checkbox is enabled. Move every corner to fit the building's perspective by clicking and dragging them to match the physical vanishing points and horizon line. Try to fit the intersections of the vertical lines to the bottom segment of your quad before moving on to complex mesh points.

 

Mesh Warping

When moving the four corners isn't enough, Mesh Warping is an essential technique that enables an internal grid mesh to reproduce complex architectural curves across multiple axes.

  • Subdividing the Grid: In the Surface Inspector under Mesh Warping, you can generate a grid by specifying the number of horizontal and vertical subdivisions.
  • Adding Custom Points: You can stick to adding extra points manually exactly where you need them. Add points with Alt + click and drag them to your desired position.
  • Removing Points & Lines: To remove a specific point, select it and press Delete. To remove an entire line, right-click on a point and select either "Remove Horizontal" or "Remove Vertical".

Tip: The fewer points you add, the easier it will be to warp correctly! Sparsely add warping points only where necessary to mimic the curvature of the facade.

Handle Adjustment & Selection Techniques

Managing complex meshes requires precision control:

  • Moving Segments & Lines: You can move a specific segment between two points by clicking on it once. Click it one more time to select the entire line across the mesh. The selected segment or line will be highlighted.
  • Multi-Selection: Click and drag a selection box over multiple mesh points, or hold Shift and click to add multiple specific points to your selection. You can move them all simultaneously.
  • Keyboard Precision: Dragging with a mouse can sometimes be too imprecise for pixel-perfect mapping. Select your point(s) and use the Arrow Keys on your keyboard to nudge them pixel by pixel. Hold Shift + Arrow Keys to move them in larger 10-pixel increments.

Bézier Curves for Curved Architecture

Standard mesh points create sharp, linear angles. To seamlessly map onto columns, domes, arches, or circular tables, you need smooth curves.

Enable them via the Bézier slider on the left panel under the mesh warping options.

Once activated, clicking on a warping point will reveal two or three tangent handles. Grab them to adjust the tangents of the curves and align your grid generator to the lines of the building.

 

 

  • Unlinking Tangents: If you have difficulties matching a sharp architectural corner, you can right-click on a point and select "Unlink Tangents" to move the handles separately. Note that this will break the tangent continuity, creating a sharp point instead of a smooth curve.

  • Mesh Precision: To add more smoothness to your surface's geometry, you can increase the "Precision" slider. This will add more polygons to your shape so your curves render softer and cleaner.

Using Masks During Warping

If an obstacle (like a person or a structural beam) is standing just in front of the mapping area, you can mask it out to prevent projection spill.

  • In the left panel, create a new mask by clicking the first mask icon.
  • Click and hold on your surface to draw a mask, which will automatically add points and Bézier curves to match your drawing. Press Enter to close the path when finished.
  • You can also edit the curves with the Bézier handles or quickly generate perfect shape boundaries using the square, triangle, and circle mask icons.

Link Input and Output Mesh

By default, the Link Input and Output Mesh is enabled. This means that if you physically drag and adjust any mesh points on the output side to match your real-world object, the input mesh will automatically mirror them, helping to keep your video undistorted.

However, if you want to warp the output shape without distorting how the media is sampled from the input—or if you want to warp the input media while leaving the output geometry alone—you must disable this link.

To disable the link and independently edit the source geometry, you must first enable Show Input Mesh.
