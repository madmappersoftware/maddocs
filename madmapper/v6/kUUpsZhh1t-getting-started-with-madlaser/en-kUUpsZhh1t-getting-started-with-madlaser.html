Getting Started with MadLaser

MadLaser Preferences

The foundational settings for laser output and safety are managed within the main MadMapper Preferences menu (Cmd + , on Mac / Ctrl + , on PC). Because lasers involve physical hardware and strict safety protocols, these settings are split between global application routing and project-specific safety limits.

Important Note: The settings detailed in the Laser tabs below are exclusive to the MadLaser extension. These menus and options will only appear in your preferences if the MadLaser extension is currently licensed and activated on your system.

Application Tab > Laser

These settings govern global audio-to-laser network protocols on your specific computer, regardless of which project file is open.

  • Enable Audio Laser (AVB / Dante): Check this box to allow MadMapper to send laser control data over standard audio network protocols.
  • Audio Device & Channels: Select your target network audio interface (e.g., NDI Audio, Dante Virtual Soundcard) and configure the Sample Rate, Buffer Size, and Audio Channels per Unit.
  • Enable Scene Markers: Toggles the transmission of scene markers over the audio network.

Project Tab > Laser

These settings contain critical safety and visualization parameters. Because they directly affect the physical output of the laser hardware, they are saved directly into your current .mad project file.

Security Settings
By default, MadMapper limits certain laser parameters to protect both your audience and your hardware.

  • Unlock Security Features: Checking this box prompts a warning regarding the dangers of high-intensity beams. Unlocking this allows you to bypass the default safety constraints.

  • Max non-moving Beam Intensity: MadMapper natively prevents static (non-moving) beams from outputting at full power. This slider dictates the maximum brightness allowed when the scanners stop moving.

Safety Warning: A full-power, static beam from a professional laser can easily ignite fires or burn skin. Never increase this limit unless you are absolutely certain the beam path is safely terminated and clear of the audience.

Enable Expert Mode Settings
Enabling Expert Mode in your Project Preferences unlocks advanced, under-the-hood parameters designed specifically for complex installations and high-end hardware.

Arming the Laser & Output Preparation

While you can design and preview your laser surfaces within the software workspace, no physical light will be emitted from your hardware until the system is armed. To see your image in the real world, you must arm the laser by toggling the Laser Output icon located in the upper right corner of the main MadMapper application window.

The Arming Warning: The first time you arm the laser output during a session, MadMapper will present a mandatory safety acknowledgment:

Please read this carefully. As the software explicitly states, you must not shoot rays towards your audience, you must comply with local regulations, and you should always use a physical safety switch (e-stop) on your hardware. Once you accept the prompt and the laser icon is activated, MadMapper will begin sending live vector data to your connected hardware.

 

Laser Outputs

With MadLaser you can create a large number of laser outputs and route them to multiple projectors in various ways. Using a form of Laser Dispatch you can spread complex content over multiple lasers with up to 100 kpps scanners for rendering high definition graphics.

 

Add laser icon:

To configure your laser, navigate to the Outputs tab and add a new laser. View the laser output inspector to configure the destination of your laser from the dropdown list.

Supported devices should automatically appear in the dropdown menu. These include:

  • ShowNET: The most seamless, plug-and-play integration. Lasers with a built-in ShowNET mainboard (such as models from the Laserworld Diode, Laserworld Purelight, Tarm, and Ray Technologies/RTI series) can connect directly via a standard LAN cable.

  • LaserCube: Recognized as one of the best and most portable plug-and-play options.

  • EtherDream: Highly supported for its 16-bit X/Y precision and network capabilities.

  • Helios USB: A popular, budget-friendly USB DAC.

  • FB3 / FB4 (Pangolin): Supported on Windows, though this requires an active Pangolin Beyond license.

  • AVB / Dante & IDN: Supported for networked environments and multi-projector setups.

Laser Surface Basics

Laser surfaces allow you to create and manage your laser outputs for your mapping projects. By adding laser surfaces, you define how shapes, generative content, or video textures are translated into real-time laser trajectories on your physical installation. Unlike standard video projection, MadMapper handles laser content in two distinct ways:

  • Native Laser Materials: Generative, shader-based materials (GLSL) that natively output mathematical vector data directly to the laser hardware. This circumvents the need for pixel-tracing, resulting in the sharpest, most scanner-efficient rendering possible.
  • Image Vectorization (Tracing): A GPU-accelerated, custom OpenCV engine that analyzes pixel-based media in real time. This allows you to trace contours and paths from movies, live camera inputs, or by sampling directly from your active video composition.

Location & Creation
Access the Laser surfaces by clicking on Laser at the top left of the interface (Shortcut: Cmd + 3). Simply click on the Laser surface of your choice in the top bar to add one.

Laser Surfaces List & Groups
Added Laser surfaces and groups will be listed on the left. You can toggle visibility, lock surfaces to prevent accidental edits, and drag surfaces into Groups to organize them by physical laser projectors.

 

 

Basic Surface Types

Laser Quad

This allows you to map vector graphics or vectorize video content within a four-point warped surface similar to a Video surface.

 

Laser Lines: Create precise, path-based line surfaces, perfect for tracing physical architecture. You can draw straight lines, Bezier curves, and split or link tangents for advanced point editing.



Laser Circle: Add circles, scale, and rotate them directly in the Stage View. Ideal for rounded architectural elements or geometric tunnels.

Laser Text: Create sharp, legible text generated as laser-optimized vector paths. Stick Fonts are highly recommended for laser use, as they trace a single line to reduce scanner flicker.


Laser Render Inspector

These are the advanced controls dictating how the software translates visual data into physical scanner movements.

  • Scan & Beam Mode: Set to Auto or manually configure.
  • Max Speed: Defines the maximum drawing speed of the laser.
  • Optimize Angles: When activated, utilizes the Angle Min, Angle Delay, and End Repeat parameters to instruct the laser scanners to dwell slightly on sharp corners, ensuring crisp geometry instead of rounded edges.
  • In Fade / Out Fade / Soft Close: Controls the blanking (turning the laser diode on/off) at the start and end of drawing paths to prevent visible tails or connecting lines.
