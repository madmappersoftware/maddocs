Hardware

If you're planning projection mapping, you'll need a projector. Most modern models will work; brightness, throw distance, and lens options simply depend on your project’s scale. We'll cover the key considerations below.

 

For light and laser mapping, you’ll find additional recommendations and best practices in the dedicated sections further down.

 

Don't forget the usual essentials: video adapters, extension cables, power solutions, and other connectors your setup requires. A well-organized toolkit makes live installations much smoother.

Projectors

Which projector should you choose?

 

The answer depends entirely on your project.  A small indoor installation requires very different equipment than lighting up Berghain's main floor or the Eiffel Tower façade.

Consider factors like projection distance, surface size and material, and the surrounding environment.

Image Resolution

A projector’s resolution determines how many pixels it displays, directly influencing image sharpness and clarity.

Higher resolution means more detailed, precise projection—particularly important for large screens or detailed content.

A minimum of 720p HD is recommended for basic use.

For sharper, more detailed projections, Full HD (1080p) is preferable, and 4K is ideal for the highest level of clarity and precision (think large surfaces or high level of details).

Brightness

Brightness is crucial when selecting a projector. Measured in lumens, it reflects the intensity of the light the device emits. Generally, more lumens produce a clearer image that remains visible even in brightly lit environments.

Contrast

Contrast is the difference between the light and dark areas in a projected image, measured as a ratio. Higher the ratios create greater distinction between light and dark areas. Keep in mind that true black doesn't exist in projection - the darkest areas appear as a very deep gray.

Refresh Rate

The refresh rate, measured in Hertz (Hz), indicates how many times per second the image updates on the screen. Standard refresh rates are 50 Hz or 60 Hz, depending on regional broadcasting standards. Higher refresh rates produce smoother motion and reduce flickering, especially noticeable with fast-moving content.

Ambient Light

Ambient light fundamentally affects projection quality and visibility.

Excessive surrounding light washes out colors, reduces contrast, and makes images appear faded or unclear.

For optimal results, minimize external light sources by darkening the environment or choose projectors with higher brightness to compensate.

Using Multiple Projectors

You may need several projectors simultaneously for your mapping, typically when projecting onto very large surfaces.

First, ensure your computer has the necessary resources, particularly graphics card capabilities. Running multiple projectors simultaneously is very demanding for entry-level computers.

If your computer doesn't have enough outputs, you will need to use an external device to add more, see Output Considerations above.

Preventing Dust

Caution! Projectors tend to accumulate dust, which can cause overheating or malfunctions.
Clean the ventilation system regularly to maintain performance and extend service life.

Transport Safety

Handle projectors carefully when moving or setting them up to maintain performance and extend lifespan. Always transport equipment in protective cases, avoid sudden shocks or vibrations, and allow the projector to cool down completely before packing. Proper handling preserves both optical quality and internal components over time.

Video wall controller

Modern Apple computers often feature limited video outputs, and Windows computers can have similar limitations, necessitating additional hardware.

To connect multiple projectors or displays, you may need:

  • Matrox TripleHead2Go

  • Datapath FX4 (professional and reliable option)

  • StarTech multi-output adapters (affordable)

Think of these devices as “video wall controller” that expand your available outputs.

MadMapper supports unlimited outputs with any license, your hardware setup is the only limitation.

 

Lighting & LED

MadMapper's native MadLight feature for light mapping supports DMX, Art-Net and sACN protocols, allowing control of LED fixtures, moving heads, and other compatible lighting devices.

DMX Protocol

DMX (Digital Multiplex) is the industry-standard protocol for controlling lighting equipment including LED fixtures, dimmers, moving heads, and other stage devices. Originally developed for theater and live events, it's now a cornerstone of interactive and audiovisual installations. DMX sends digital signals for precise parameter control.

Art-Net, sACN Protocol

These network-based protocols transmit DMX data over standard Ethernet.

They make large-scale lighting setups more flexible and manageable, sending multiple DMX universes through a single network cable.

Unlike traditional DMX connections limited by cable length and channel count, they distribute data efficiently across long distances and complex installations.

Broadcast vs Unicast

Broadcast (preferred for small installations): transfer data to all network device simultaneously. Easy to configure.

Unicast (preferred for bigger installations including a lof of DMX universes): transfer data to each network device independently with much better performances, sending data only where needed. Requires extra configuration.

Multicast (sACN) is often preferred for large installations, it reduces the network traffic and ensures a simultaneous signal reception on all devices.

LED by garageCube

Since its successful Kickstarter launch, LED by garageCube has remained user-friendly and highly adaptable, designed for beginners and professionals alike. Each unit assembles easily using sturdy yet lightweight aluminum supports, making it simple to transport, set up, and reconfigure.

 

The system offers an ideal introduction to DMX lighting, while remaining perfectly suited for scalable, upgradeable installations. Expand your setup at any time by adding LED bars in different formats (available in RGB or Pure White) or incorporate DMX bulbs.

 

While LED by garageCube is compatible with any software supporting the DMX protocol, it truly shines with MadMapper. Developed alongside MadMapper’s MadLight feature, the system integrates seamlessly, allowing precise, flexible, and enjoyable light design and control.

While this hardware works seamlessly with MadMapper, you can use any other DMX-compatible equipment that fits your project needs.

USB-DMX Controllers

To enable communication between your computer (and MadMapper) and your DMX hardware, you need a dedicated controller to interpret the transmitted signal. It's mandatory.

 

A USB-to-DMX controller is typically a small plug-and-play box controlling a few universes. For instance, the garageCube controller (no driver required for MadMapper) handles up to 512 DMX channels (8 meters of -any- LED by garageCube).

Since MadMapper 4, multiple usb-to-dmx controllers can be used simultaneously by adding multiple DMX Router Modules.

That being said, we recommend an Art-Net controller for serious installations.

Art-Net Controllers

Art-Net protocol transmits data over Ethernet networks - ideal for large-scale, synchronized lighting projects. Art-Net controllers are much larger and more capable than usb-dmx controllers.

 

They handle multiple universes; for example, the garageCube controller can manage up to 8 DMX universes (64 meters of LED by garageCube).

Note that web-based admin setup is a standard for such controllers.

It's once again the perfect companion for large installations requiring multiple DMX universes management.

Moreover, it's considered the most reliable option as well as the most versatile one since it allows to control other DMX devices at the same time.

Laser DAC

Since MadMapper 5, you can extend functionality with extensions such as MadLaser, which enable laser mapping within the MadMapper workflow, including realtime control.

 

You'll need a compatible Laser DAC, the bridge between software and laser hardware.

Compatible DACs are listed below, with more coming.

LaserCube

We support LaserCube Wifi, Ethernet and USB on all platforms.

Prefer Ethernet over WiFi when both are available.

 

https://www.laseros.com

Note that the USB version is Mac only.

Ether Dream

Ether Dream (ethernet - 16 bits XY) needs a clean network without other traffic.

Don’t mix Art-Net and Ether Dream on the same network or you’ll experience disconnections.

 

https://ether-dream.com

You can safely put a bunch of Ether Dream on the same network though.

ShowNET

MadMapper supports the ShowNET protocol, so any LaserWorld projector with ShowNET works directly with just a network cable (Auto IP works fine). For best performance, put ShowNET devices on a network without other traffic.

 

https://www.laserworld.com

Check your laser device documentation to see how to setup the ShowNET dip switch corresponding to your network mode (DHCP / Auto-IP or static IP).

If your ShowNET device appears as a destination in MadMapper’s Laser Output list but fails to connect (status remains Disconnected), check that you do not have multiple network interfaces using the same IP address range (e.g. 192.168.0.xxx / 255.255.255.0).

If another interface (such as Wi-Fi or a secondary network adapter) is using the same IP range, either disable that interface, assign it a different IP range, or switch the ShowNET pins to Auto-IP mode. In this case, set your computer’s network interface to DHCP as well, so both devices automatically assign themselves Auto-IP addresses (169.254.xxx.xxx / 255.255.0.0).

Recommend Laser Devices for Testing

It depends on the budget and intended projects.

For graphics: needs accurate scanning, precise and small beam, low divergence and good diodes alignment.

For video projection with laser overlay: a small beam (low divergence) works better.

For architectural mapping, atmospheric effects, clubbing or festivals: prioritize power over sharpness.

For museum installations: choose a low power with sharp beam.

An example: the LaserWorld Tarm 3W offers good whites (no need to reduce blue by 50%), adequate sharpness and 3W sufficient for atmospheric effects.

Many laser devices are comparable in quality. LaserWorld is notable for having an integrated Ethernet DAC (ShowNET), eliminating the need for an external DAC (Ethernet or USB to ILDA).

Safety Recommendations

Lasers are powerful and dangerous, handle them carefully with audience safety in mind.

Comply with local regulations.

MadMapper cannot be held responsible for misuse of laser equipment.

Above all, never project laser beams toward the audience. Always use a hardware safety switch and define a dedicated safety zone.

Also, protect your laser hardware by ensuring the scan rate remains within safe limits.
