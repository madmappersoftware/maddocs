Hardware

If you're planning projection mapping, you'll need a projector. Most modern models will work; brightness, throw distance, and lens options simply depend on your project’s scale. We'll cover the key considerations below.

 

For light and laser mapping, you’ll find additional recommendations and best practices in the dedicated sections further down.

 

Don't forget the usual essentials: video adapters, extension cables, power solutions, and other connectors your setup requires. A well-organized toolkit makes live installations much smoother.

Projectors

Which projector should you choose?

 

The answer depends entirely on your project.  A small indoor installation requires very different equipment than lighting up Berghain's main floor or the Eiffel Tower façade.

Consider factors like projection distance, surface size and material, and the surrounding environment.

Image Resolution

A projector’s resolution determines how many pixels it displays, directly influencing image sharpness and clarity.

Higher resolution means more detailed, precise projection—particularly important for large screens or detailed content.

A minimum of 720p HD is recommended for basic use.

For sharper, more detailed projections, Full HD (1080p) is preferable, and 4K is ideal for the highest level of clarity and precision (think large surfaces or high level of details).

Brightness

Brightness is crucial when selecting a projector. Measured in lumens, it reflects the intensity of the light the device emits. Generally, more lumens produce a clearer image that remains visible even in brightly lit environments.

Contrast

Contrast is the difference between the light and dark areas in a projected image, measured as a ratio. Higher the ratios create greater distinction between light and dark areas. Keep in mind that true black doesn't exist in projection - the darkest areas appear as a very deep gray.

Refresh Rate

The refresh rate, measured in Hertz (Hz), indicates how many times per second the image updates on the screen. Standard refresh rates are 50 Hz or 60 Hz, depending on regional broadcasting standards. Higher refresh rates produce smoother motion and reduce flickering, especially noticeable with fast-moving content.

Ambient Light

Ambient light fundamentally affects projection quality and visibility.

Excessive surrounding light washes out colors, reduces contrast, and makes images appear faded or unclear.

For optimal results, minimize external light sources by darkening the environment or choose projectors with higher brightness to compensate.

Using Multiple Projectors

You may need several projectors simultaneously for your mapping, typically when projecting onto very large surfaces.

First, ensure your computer has the necessary resources, particularly graphics card capabilities. Running multiple projectors simultaneously is very demanding for entry-level computers.

If your computer doesn't have enough outputs, you will need to use an external device to add more, see Output Considerations above.

Preventing Dust

Caution! Projectors tend to accumulate dust, which can cause overheating or malfunctions.
Clean the ventilation system regularly to maintain performance and extend service life.

Transport Safety

When moving or setting up projectors, it’s important to handle them with care to maintain their performance and extend their lifespan.

Always transport the equipment in protective cases, avoid sudden shocks or vibrations, and allow the projector to cool down completely before packing it.

Proper handling and regular precautions help preserve both the optical quality and the internal components over time.

Lighting & LED

MadMapper's MadLight native feature dedicated to light mapping seamlessly supports DMX, Art-Net or sACN protocols, allowing you to control LED fixtures, moving heads, and other compatible lighting devices.

DMX Protocol

DMX (short for Digital Multiplex) is the industry-standard protocol used to control lighting equipment such as LED fixtures, dimmers, moving heads, and other stage devices. It was originally developed for theater and live events but has since become a cornerstone of interactive and audiovisual installations. DMX works by sending digital signals, allowing precise control over parameters.

Art-Net, sACN Protocol

These are network-based protocols designed to transmit DMX data over standard Ethernet.

It was created to make large-scale lighting setups more flexible and easier to manage, allowing you to send multiple DMX universes through a single network cable.

Unlike traditional DMX connections, which are limited by cable length and channel count, they let you distribute data efficiently across long distances and complex installations.

Broadcast vs Unicast

Broadcast (preferred for small installations) is transferring data to all network device simultaneously and is easy to configure. Unicast (preferred for bigger installations including a lof of DMX universes) is transferring data to each network device independently and have much better performances by sending data only where is needed. Unicast need an extra configuration.

Multicast (think sACN) is often preferred for large installations because it reduces network traffic and ensures all devices receive the signal simultaneously.

LED by garageCube

Since its successful Kickstarter launch, LED by garageCube has remained a user-friendly and highly adaptable LED system, designed for both beginners and professionals. Each unit can be assembled easily using sturdy yet lightweight aluminum supports, making it simple to transport, set up, and reconfigure for different projects.

 

The system offers an ideal introduction to the world of DMX lighting, while remaining perfectly suited for scalable, upgradeable installations. You can expand your setup at any time by adding LED bars in different formats (available in RGB or Pure White versions) or even incorporate DMX bulbs.

 

Although LED by garageCube is compatible with any software supporting the DMX protocol, it truly shines when paired with MadMapper. Developed alongside MadMapper’s MadLight feature, the LED system integrates seamlessly with it, allowing you to design and control light with precision, flexibility, and a whole lot of fun.

While this hardware works seamlessly with MadMapper, you are of course free to use any other DMX-compatible equipment that fits your project.

Usb-Dmx Controllers

To enable communication between your computer (and MadMapper) and your DMX hardware, you’ll need a dedicated controller. This device will basically interpret the transmitted signal. It's mandatory!

 

A USB to DMX controller is generally a small plug-and-play box allowing you to control a few universes. For instance, the garageCube one (no driver required to use it with MadMapper) can handle up to 512 DMX channels (= 8 meters of -any- LED by garageCube).

Since MadMapper 4, you can use multiple usb-to-dmx controllers simultaneously by adding multiple DMX Router Modules.

Although we would always recommend an Art-Net Controller for serious installations.

Art-Net Controllers

Art-Net protocol transmits data over Ethernet networks. It’s ideal for large-scale and synchronized lighting projects. Art-Net controllers are much larger and capable devices than a usb-dmx ones.

 

They can handle multiple universes, for example the garageCube one can manage up to 8 DMX universes (= 64 meters of LED by garageCube).

Note that web page admin setup is a standard for such controllers.

This is again the perfect companion for large installations requiring to manage multiple DMX universes.

It's also considered the most reliable option as well as the most versatile one since it allows you to control other DMX devices at the same time.

Laser DAC

Since MadMapper 5, you can extend the software's functionality with extensions such as MadLaser.

MadLaser allows you to do laser mapping while benefiting from the MadMapper workflow, including realtime control.

 

You will need a compatible Laser DAC though. DACs being the bridge between the software and the laser hardware.

Compatible DACs are listed below. More to come!

LaserCube

We support LaserCube Wifi, Ethernet and usb on all platforms.

Although both protocols are supported, prefer Ethernet over Wi-Fi.

 

https://www.laseros.com

Note that the USB version is Mac only.

Ether Dream

Ether Dream (ethernet - 16 bits XY) needs to run on a clean network without other traffic.

For instance, don’t mix Art-Net and Ether Dream on the same network or you’ll get disconnections.

 

https://ether-dream.com

You can safely put a bunch of Ether Dream on the same network though.

ShowNET

MadMapper supports ShowNET protocol - DAC, so any LaserWorld projector with ShowNET will work directly with just a network cable (Auto IP works fine).

Also prefer putting your ShowNET devices on a network without other traffic for best smoothness.

 

https://www.laserworld.com

Check your laser device documentation to see how to set the ShowNET dip switch for the network mode you’re using (DHCP / Auto-IP or static IP).

If your ShowNET device appears as a destination in MadMapper Laser Output destinations but does not connect (status keeps “Disconnected”), verify that you don’t have multiple network interfaces on the same IP address range (ie 192.168.0.xxx / 255.255.255.0).

If you have a wifi or another network in the same IP range, either disable your other network adapter or change its IP address, or change the ShowNET pins for “Auto-IP” mode (& your computer interface in DHCP so your system setups an AutoIP address too (169.254.xxx.xxx / 255.255.0.0).

Recommend Laser device for testing

It depends on the budget and possible projects.

For graphics (needs accurate scanning, precise and small beam, so low divergence and good diodes alignment) or for atmospheric.

For video projection + laser on top, a small beam (low divergence) may be better.

For architectural mapping, atmospheric, clubbing or festival, we would prefer power over sharpness.

But for a museum piece for instance it’s better to choose a low power/sharp beam one.

An exemple would be the LaserWorld Tarm 3W : good whites (not need to lower blue by 50%), sharp enough and 3W is ok for atmospheric.

Note that lot of laser devices look close in term of quality.

LaserWorld is nice because they have an ethernet DAC inside (ShowNET) so you don't need an external DAC (ethernet or USB -> ILDA).

Safety Recommendations

Lasers are powerful and dangerous, hence they should be handled with care and safety for the audience in mind.

So please comply with your local regulations.

Please note that MadMapper cannot be hold responsible for misusage of laser equipment.

Above all, don’t shoot rays towards your audience, always use a safety switch on your hardware and specify a safety area.

Avoid burning your laser hardware by limiting the laser scan rate too.

Other Hardware

What else could you possibly need?


For example, a MIDI controller for your live performances, or a capture card such as a BlackMagic's DeckLink.

 

As for controllers, as long as it supports MIDI, you're good to go.

We don't have any particular models or formats to recommend. It all depends on your tastes and budget.

Today's MIDI controllers come in a wide variety of models, with knobs, faders, or more pad-oriented designs, and in all price ranges.
