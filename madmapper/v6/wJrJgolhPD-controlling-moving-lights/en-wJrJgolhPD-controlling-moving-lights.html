Controlling Moving Lights

MadMapper's fixture engine is not limited to LED strips; it is fully capable of driving intelligent fixtures, moving heads, and complex DMX devices (like fog machines or the Minuit Une IVL Dice), but the logic you are used to to control the devices will be slightly different from traditional DMX consoles.

Instead of relying on a traditional programmer with faders and palettes for every parameter, MadMapper drives the color and intensity of the fixture by sampling your video content, while generating custom UI sliders for the mechanical features.

To control moving lights:

  1. Create a custom fixture definition with a 1x1 pixel grid.
  2. Set the Pixel Type to Custom.
  3. Map the manufacturer's DMX sheet to the channels. Assign the color parameters to R, G, B types (so they sample the video map).
  4. Assign the Pan, Tilt, Strobe, and Gobo parameters to the Slider type.
  5. In your MadMapper workspace, these Sliders will now appear in the Fixture Inspector, allowing you to manually aim the moving heads while their colors dynamically react to your video content!

Tip: After you have created a slider, you can animate it with keyframes in the Timelines tab, or connect it to Oscillator modules created under the Modules tab to generate automatic, continuous movement.

Real-World Example: PAR 6-Channel

For standard pixel mapping with addressable RGB LED pixels, a fixture will not require a complex definition. But this example shows how MadMapper fixtures can be customized to work with non-standard fixtures like an LED PAR can with additional color diodes, such as Amber and UV (Ultraviolet).

This fixture is a 6-channel LED PAR. It uses a 1×1 pixel grid with the Pixel Type set to "Custom" and a Pixel Size of 6 channels. The first four channels are assigned to standard color types (R, G, B, and W) so they can directly sample the video map. The remaining two channels are dedicated to the Amber and UV diodes.

By setting these last two channels to the "Slider" type and labeling them "Amber" and "UV", MadMapper automatically generates dedicated sliders for them at the bottom of the Fixture Inspector. This allows the operator to manually mix in Amber and UV light independently while the main RGBW channels dynamically react to the video content.

 

 

 

Tips for Custom Fixtures

Naming conventions — Use clear, descriptive channel labels. Include "Fine" in the label for the LSB channel of 16-bit pairs so operators can quickly identify coarse/fine relationships.

Modes — Many fixtures have multiple DMX modes with different channel counts. Create a separate definition for each mode and include the mode in the product name (e.g., "Minuit Une IVL Dice Mode 1").

Testing — After creating a fixture definition, use the DMX Monitor panel (Option+Cmd+M / Alt+Ctrl+M) to verify that channels are mapping correctly and values are reaching the expected DMX addresses.

Color channel types — Use the correct R/G/B/W/C/M/Y types for color channels rather than Slider. This allows MadMapper's color engine to properly drive them with pixel-mapped media, materials, and color pickers.

Cross-universe fixtures — If your fixture spans more than 512 channels or if its DMX address places it near the end of a universe, enable avoidCrossUniversePixels to prevent a single pixel's channels from being split across two universes.

Backup — Keep your custom .mmfl files in a shared location or version control. Fixture definitions are stored within MadMapper's library, but having external backups ensures you can quickly rebuild on a new machine.
