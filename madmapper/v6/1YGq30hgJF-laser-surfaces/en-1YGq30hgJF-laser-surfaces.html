Laser Surfaces

Arming the Laser & Output Preparation

While you can design and preview your laser surfaces within the software workspace, no physical light will be emitted from your hardware until the system is armed.

To see your image in the real world, you must arm the laser by toggling the Laser Output icon located in the upper right corner of the main MadMapper application window.

 

Crucial Pre-Flight Check: Before arming the laser, it is highly recommended that you configure and enable any necessary area limitations or "safety masks" within the Laser Output menu to physically block beams from hitting unsafe areas. (Note: A detailed breakdown of projector-specific safety zones and hardware routing will be covered in the separate Outputs documentation page).

 

The Arming Warning The first time you arm the laser output during a session, MadMapper will present a mandatory safety acknowledgment:

Please read this carefully. As the software explicitly states:

  • Lasers are powerful and dangerous, hence they should be handled with care and safety for the audience in mind.

  • Please comply with your local regulations.

  • MadMapper / GarageCube / 1024 architecture cannot be held responsible for misusage of laser equipment.

  • Don't shoot rays towards your audience; always use a physical safety switch (e-stop) on your hardware.

  • Avoid burning your laser hardware by limiting the laser scan rate and specifying a scan area.

Once you accept the prompt and the laser icon is activated, MadMapper will begin sending live vector data to your connected hardware.

Basics

Laser surfaces allow you to create and manage your laser outputs for your mapping projects. By adding laser surfaces, you define how shapes, generative content, or video textures are translated into real-time laser trajectories on your physical installation.

Unlike standard video projection, MadMapper handles laser content in two distinct ways:

  • Native Laser Materials: Generative, shader-based materials (GLSL) that natively output mathematical vector data directly to the laser hardware. This circumvents the need for pixel-tracing, resulting in the sharpest, most scanner-efficient rendering possible.

  • Image Vectorization (Tracing): A GPU-accelerated, custom OpenCV engine that analyzes pixel-based media in real time. This allows you to trace contours and paths from movies, live camera inputs, or by sampling directly from your active video composition.

Laser Surface Types

Laser Quad

This allows you to map vector graphics or vectorize video content within a four-point warped surface similar to a Video surface:

 

Laser Lines

It allows you to create precise, path-based line surfaces, perfect for tracing physical architecture. The drawing behavior changes based on your input:

  • Straight Lines: Select the Laser Line tool, then click points on the Output Stage to draw sharp angles. Press Return or Esc to finish the path.

  • Bezier Curves: Click and drag on the Output Stage to draw free-form, curved lines. Once you release the mouse, MadMapper will automatically simplify the stroke into adjustable anchor points.

  • Adding Points: To add a new anchor point to an existing line, simply double-click anywhere on the path.

Advanced Point Editing Once a line is created, you can right-click any specific anchor point to access a context menu for advanced path editing. The available options adapt based on the type of point selected:

  • Continue Path from Here (C): Resumes drawing the line starting from the selected end point.

  • Enable / Disable Bezier (B): Toggles the selected point between a sharp, rigid corner and a smooth, curved point.

  • Split Path Here (S): Breaks a single, continuous line into two entirely separate line segments at the selected point.

  • Link Tangents (L): (Available only on Bezier points) Locks the two Bezier handles together so that adjusting the curve on one side perfectly mirrors the other. Unlinking them allows you to adjust each side independently for sharp, customized curves.

   

Tip: Similar to standard vector drawing software, whenever you select a point with Bezier enabled, two directional handles will appear. Click and drag these handles to precisely adjust the arc and trajectory of your laser curve.

Laser Circle

Add circles as a laser surface, then adjust (with the four handle points), scale, and rotate your circle(s) directly in the Stage View. Just like with video surfaces, the perspective can be scaled using the center handle. This surface type is ideal for mapping rounded architectural elements or generating geometric tunnel effects:

 

Laser Text

Create sharp, legible text that is natively generated as laser-optimized vector paths, including support for single-line stick fonts. (See the Laser Surface Inspector below for more details on text formatting).

Laser 3D

Import and calibrate 3D objects in the .OBJ format directly to your laser output. This surface type allows you to import standard .OBJ files and project them as high-definition volumetric wireframes: (See the 3D Surface Inspector below for more details).

Laser Surface Inspectors

The Laser Surface Inspector, located below the list of surfaces, displays the settings and parameters of the selected surface (highlighted in the surfaces list). The available settings vary depending on the specific type of Laser surface you are working with.

Media, Appearance, FX, and Output

For these standard formatting and routing parameters, please refer to the general settings detailed in the Video Surfaces Documentation. They behave identically across both video and laser workflows.

 

Laser Render

 

Advanced controls dictating how the software translates visual data into physical scanner movements:

  • Scan & Beam Mode: Set to Auto or manually configure.

  • Max Speed: Defines the maximum drawing speed of the laser.

  • Optimize Angles: When activated, utilizes the Angle Min, Angle Delay, and End Repeat parameters to instruct the laser scanners to dwell slightly on sharp corners, ensuring crisp geometry instead of rounded edges.

  • In Fade / Out Fade / Soft Close: Controls the blanking (turning the laser diode on/off) at the start and end of drawing paths to prevent visible tails or connecting lines.

Process

(Applies only to Laser Quads, Circles, and 3D Objects) Dictates how content is vectorized into laser paths. The options change based on the surface type:

(Image for Quads/Circles)  (Image for 3D)

 

  • For Quads & Circles: Controls real-time image tracing. Set the Mode (e.g., Find Paths), adjust the tracing Threshold, toggle Use Color, and refine the trace using Thickness, Denoizing, and Max Res to balance detail against scanner workload.

  • For 3D Objects: Controls volumetric rendering. Set the Mode (e.g., Depth), and fine-tune the Sensibility and Definition to control how much geometric wireframe detail is sent to the laser.

Path Filtering

(Applies only to Laser Quads, Circles, and 3D Objects) Allows you to filter out unwanted geometry based on physical length:

  • Min / Max Length: Defines the length limits as a percentage of the media dimension. For example, setting Min Length to 10% on a 1024x1024 video will automatically hide any generated polylines that are shorter than roughly 11 pixels, which is excellent for instantly removing visual noise from traced video.

Path Limits

(Applies only to Laser Quads, Circles, and 3D Objects) A critical safety and optimization tool to prevent overwhelming the laser scanners with too much complex geometry:

  • Count: Defines the absolute maximum number of polylines to keep and send to the laser. (Pro-tip: Press the Option (Mac) / Alt (PC) key while dragging the slider for fine-tuning).

  • Mode: If the vectorization exceeds the maximum Count limit, this parameter defines which paths to prioritize and keep (e.g., Keep Longest or Keep Shortest).

Mesh Warping

(Applies only to Laser Quads, Lines, and Text) An essential technique that applies a grid mesh to warp your laser output. Activate Bezier Mesh Warping to use curves to fit your laser perfectly to complex physical surfaces. (See Video Mesh Warping for more details).

Soft-Edge

(Applies only to Laser Quads) Extremely useful when overlapping multiple laser projectors. This allows you to blend and fade the intersecting laser zones smoothly. (See Video Mesh Warping for more details).

 

Note: If you Shift + Select multiple surfaces at once, the Inspector will only display the common parameters shared by all selected surfaces. Additionally, remember that the percentage of a setting on an individual surface is always multiplied by the master settings of its parent Group.

Laser Text Settings

When a Laser Text surface is selected, this dedicated panel appears for typographic control:

  • Text Box & Aspect Ratio: Type your characters and define the overall proportions of the text's bounding box on the output stage.

  • Font Type & Font: Choose your typeface. You can select standard system fonts, or switch to Stick Font. (Tip: Stick Fonts are highly recommended for laser use, as they trace a single line to reduce scanner flicker).

  • Formatting: Adjust italics, horizontal/vertical alignment, and reading direction.

  • Adjust Font Size To Fit: Automatically scale your typography to perfectly fill the defined bounding box.

Laser Lines Settings

When a Laser Lines surface is selected, access tools for managing your custom vector paths (similar to Video Lines):

  • Tools & Layers: Manage, select, and organize the individual line segments drawn within the surface.

  • Min Points: Defines the minimum number of ILDA points to generate for the selected path. (Pro-tip: Press the Option (Mac) / Alt (PC) key while dragging the slider for fine-tuning).

  • Revert Path / SVG IO: Quickly revert edits, or use Import/Export SVG to load external vector artwork or save your custom stage traces.

Laser 3D Settings

When a Laser 3D surface is selected, access tools for projecting volumetric shapes (Similar to Video 3D surfaces):

  • 3D Model: Select a built-in primitive shape (like a Box) or import your own .OBJ file. Adjust Face Culling to hide the back sides of objects, drastically optimizing laser rendering.

  • Texturing: Define how textures and colors map to the Object UVs.

  • Calibration: Use the Calibrate toggle and Fix Position button to accurately align the 3D rendering with real-world physical objects.

Display

Specifies what is visually rendered in the software preview. This is an essential diagnostic tool used for understanding the internal vectorization process and figuring out how your source media creation or encoding can be improved for better laser output.

  • Mode: Use the dropdown to switch the workspace preview between different stages of the rendering pipeline. Options include:

    • Output Polylines: Displays the final, generated vector paths that will be sent to the physical laser.

    • Normals / Depth / Color: Visualizes specific data channels extracted from the source media.

    • Processed Image / Thinned Image: Shows the intermediate steps of the computer vision engine as it breaks down standard video pixels into traceable lines.

Monitor

 

Displays real-time errors and statistical information about the vectorization process. Use this panel to keep an eye on the internal math (like Initial Polyline Count vs. Recomposed Polylines) to ensure your composition isn't generating an unsafe or overwhelming amount of geometry for the hardware scanners to process.

Advanced Laser Preferences For global hardware routing (such as AVB/Dante audio-to-laser networks) and critical project safety limits (including non-moving beam intensity and Expert Mode scan rates), please refer to the laser-specific settings detailed in the MadLaser Preferences documentation.

 

Expert Mode Surface Features

When Expert Mode is enabled in your Project Preferences, the Laser Surface inspector unlocks several advanced, under-the-hood parameters designed for complex installations and high-end hardware.

  • RGB Samples (Laser Lines & Text): Increases the maximum allowable color sampling resolution up to 64k (default is 8k). Warning: 64k samples will have a heavy impact on software performance.

  • Expanded Speed Ranges: Increases the maximum allowed values for the Max Speed and Fixed Speed parameters up to 5.0 (normally restricted to 3.0 to protect entry-level scanners).

  • Start Repeat: Unhides a dedicated parameter allowing you to add extra dwell points specifically at the starting point of a path.

  • Advanced Video Vectorization: Unlocks deeper, experimental variables when converting standard video or images into laser paths (using "Find Paths" or "Find Contours").

  • Maximum Input Dimension: Allows the video vectorization engine to process media up to 16k resolution (normally limited to 8k). While real-time performance may degrade at 16k, this is an incredibly powerful feature for converting massive files into non-real-time ILDA exports via the Timeline.

Laser Drawing Order and Optimization

Does the layer order of Laser Surfaces determine their drawing order? No. Unlike video surfaces where the layer stack determines what is rendered on top, the Laser Output receives all paths from all active laser surfaces simultaneously. The software then mathematically calculates the most efficient rendering order to optimize "blanking time" and minimize the physical distance the scanner needs to travel. This ensures the fastest, cleanest global scan possible.
