Laser (MadLaser)

Getting Started with MadLaser

MadLaser Preferences

The foundational settings for laser output and safety are managed within the main MadMapper Preferences menu (Cmd + , on Mac / Ctrl + , on PC). Because lasers involve physical hardware and strict safety protocols, these settings are split between global application routing and project-specific safety limits.

Important Note: The settings detailed in the Laser tabs below are exclusive to the MadLaser extension. These menus and options will only appear in your preferences if the MadLaser extension is currently licensed and activated on your system.

Application Tab > Laser

These settings govern global audio-to-laser network protocols on your specific computer, regardless of which project file is open.

  • Enable Audio Laser (AVB / Dante): Check this box to allow MadMapper to send laser control data over standard audio network protocols.
  • Audio Device & Channels: Select your target network audio interface (e.g., NDI Audio, Dante Virtual Soundcard) and configure the Sample Rate, Buffer Size, and Audio Channels per Unit.
  • Enable Scene Markers: Toggles the transmission of scene markers over the audio network.

Project Tab > Laser

These settings contain critical safety and visualization parameters. Because they directly affect the physical output of the laser hardware, they are saved directly into your current .mad project file.

Security Settings
By default, MadMapper limits certain laser parameters to protect both your audience and your hardware.

  • Unlock Security Features: Checking this box prompts a warning regarding the dangers of high-intensity beams. Unlocking this allows you to bypass the default safety constraints.

  • Max non-moving Beam Intensity: MadMapper natively prevents static (non-moving) beams from outputting at full power. This slider dictates the maximum brightness allowed when the scanners stop moving.

Safety Warning: A full-power, static beam from a professional laser can easily ignite fires or burn skin. Never increase this limit unless you are absolutely certain the beam path is safely terminated and clear of the audience.

Enable Expert Mode Settings
Enabling Expert Mode in your Project Preferences unlocks advanced, under-the-hood parameters designed specifically for complex installations and high-end hardware.

Arming the Laser & Output Preparation

While you can design and preview your laser surfaces within the software workspace, no physical light will be emitted from your hardware until the system is armed. To see your image in the real world, you must arm the laser by toggling the Laser Output icon located in the upper right corner of the main MadMapper application window.

The Arming Warning: The first time you arm the laser output during a session, MadMapper will present a mandatory safety acknowledgment:

Please read this carefully. As the software explicitly states, you must not shoot rays towards your audience, you must comply with local regulations, and you should always use a physical safety switch (e-stop) on your hardware. Once you accept the prompt and the laser icon is activated, MadMapper will begin sending live vector data to your connected hardware.

 

Laser Outputs

With MadLaser you can create a large number of laser outputs and route them to multiple projectors in various ways. Using a form of Laser Dispatch you can spread complex content over multiple lasers with up to 100 kpps scanners for rendering high definition graphics.

 

Add laser icon:

To configure your laser, navigate to the Outputs tab and add a new laser. View the laser output inspector to configure the destination of your laser from the dropdown list.

Supported devices should automatically appear in the dropdown menu. These include:

  • ShowNET: The most seamless, plug-and-play integration. Lasers with a built-in ShowNET mainboard (such as models from the Laserworld Diode, Laserworld Purelight, Tarm, and Ray Technologies/RTI series) can connect directly via a standard LAN cable.

  • LaserCube: Recognized as one of the best and most portable plug-and-play options.

  • EtherDream: Highly supported for its 16-bit X/Y precision and network capabilities.

  • Helios USB: A popular, budget-friendly USB DAC.

  • FB3 / FB4 (Pangolin): Supported on Windows, though this requires an active Pangolin Beyond license.

  • AVB / Dante & IDN: Supported for networked environments and multi-projector setups.

Laser Surface Basics

Laser surfaces allow you to create and manage your laser outputs for your mapping projects. By adding laser surfaces, you define how shapes, generative content, or video textures are translated into real-time laser trajectories on your physical installation. Unlike standard video projection, MadMapper handles laser content in two distinct ways:

  • Native Laser Materials: Generative, shader-based materials (GLSL) that natively output mathematical vector data directly to the laser hardware. This circumvents the need for pixel-tracing, resulting in the sharpest, most scanner-efficient rendering possible.
  • Image Vectorization (Tracing): A GPU-accelerated, custom OpenCV engine that analyzes pixel-based media in real time. This allows you to trace contours and paths from movies, live camera inputs, or by sampling directly from your active video composition.

Location & Creation
Access the Laser surfaces by clicking on Laser at the top left of the interface (Shortcut: Cmd + 3). Simply click on the Laser surface of your choice in the top bar to add one.

Laser Surfaces List & Groups
Added Laser surfaces and groups will be listed on the left. You can toggle visibility, lock surfaces to prevent accidental edits, and drag surfaces into Groups to organize them by physical laser projectors.

 

 

Basic Surface Types

Laser Quad

This allows you to map vector graphics or vectorize video content within a four-point warped surface similar to a Video surface.

 

Laser Lines: Create precise, path-based line surfaces, perfect for tracing physical architecture. You can draw straight lines, Bezier curves, and split or link tangents for advanced point editing.



Laser Circle: Add circles, scale, and rotate them directly in the Stage View. Ideal for rounded architectural elements or geometric tunnels.

Laser Text: Create sharp, legible text generated as laser-optimized vector paths. Stick Fonts are highly recommended for laser use, as they trace a single line to reduce scanner flicker.


Laser Render Inspector

These are the advanced controls dictating how the software translates visual data into physical scanner movements.

  • Scan & Beam Mode: Set to Auto or manually configure.
  • Max Speed: Defines the maximum drawing speed of the laser.
  • Optimize Angles: When activated, utilizes the Angle Min, Angle Delay, and End Repeat parameters to instruct the laser scanners to dwell slightly on sharp corners, ensuring crisp geometry instead of rounded edges.
  • In Fade / Out Fade / Soft Close: Controls the blanking (turning the laser diode on/off) at the start and end of drawing paths to prevent visible tails or connecting lines.

ILDA Files

The ILDA format (.ild) is the universal standard for sharing and playing back pre-rendered laser graphics and animations. MadMapper fully supports importing and exporting ILDA files to integrate seamlessly with other laser workflows.

Importing ILDA Content

You can import .ild files directly into your Media Bin just like a standard video file. Once imported, simply drag and drop the ILDA file onto any active Laser Surface to play back the pre-rendered vector animation. Because ILDA files already contain exact X/Y coordinate and color data, they are incredibly efficient and bypass the need for real-time image tracing.

Exporting ILDA Content

If you use MadMapper's powerful generative engine to create custom laser shows, you can bake that content down into a static file for use on standalone media players or other laser software.

  • Via the Timeline: You can export sequences directly to ILDA via the Timeline export options.
  • High-Resolution Vectorization (Expert Mode): When Expert Mode is enabled, the video vectorization engine can process media up to 16k resolution. While real-time performance will degrade at 16k, this is an incredibly powerful feature for executing non-real-time conversions of massive video files into high-resolution ILDA exports.

Real-Time Video Vectorization

One of MadMapper's most powerful features is its ability to convert standard, pixel-based video content into physical laser vectors in real-time. This is handled primarily through the Laser Quad surface.

 

Process Inspector

This panel dictates how standard video is broken down and traced by the computer vision engine.

  • Set the Mode (e.g., Find Paths or Find Contours).
  • Adjust the tracing Threshold and toggle Use Color.
  • Refine the trace using Thickness, Denoizing, and Max Res to balance visual detail against the physical workload of the laser scanners.

Path Filtering & Limits

These are critical safety and optimization tools to prevent overwhelming the laser scanners with too much complex geometry.

  • Min / Max Length: Defines length limits as a percentage. Setting Min Length to 10% will automatically hide any generated polylines that are shorter than roughly 11 pixels—excellent for instantly removing visual noise from traced video.

  • Count: Defines the absolute maximum number of polylines to keep and send to the laser.
  • Mode: If the vectorization exceeds the maximum Count limit, this parameter defines which paths to prioritize and keep (e.g., Keep Longest or Keep Shortest).

Display & Monitor Diagnostics

Use the Display mode to switch the workspace preview between different stages of the rendering pipeline (e.g., Output Polylines vs. Processed Image). This helps you understand how the engine is breaking down the pixels.

The Monitor displays real-time errors and statistical information about the vectorization process. Use this panel to keep an eye on the internal math to ensure your composition isn't generating an unsafe amount of geometry for the hardware scanners to process.

3D Object Rendering for Laser

The Laser 3D surface allows you to import standard .OBJ files and project them as high-definition volumetric wireframes.

Laser 3D Settings

  • 3D Model: Select a built-in primitive shape or import your own .OBJ file. Adjust Face Culling to hide the back sides of objects, drastically optimizing laser rendering by preventing the scanner from drawing lines you can't see.
  • Texturing: Define how textures and colors map to the Object UVs.
  • Calibration: Use the Calibrate toggle and Fix Position button to accurately align the 3D rendering with real-world physical objects.

Process (For 3D Objects)

Controls the volumetric rendering output.

  • Set the Mode (e.g., Depth), and fine-tune the Sensibility and Definition to control exactly how much geometric wireframe detail is sent to the laser.

Laser Warping & Soft Edge

Mesh Warping

(Applies to Laser Quads, Lines, and Text)
An essential technique that applies a grid mesh to warp your laser output. Activate Bezier Mesh Warping to use curves to fit your laser perfectly to complex physical surfaces.

Soft-Edge

(Applies to Laser Quads)
Extremely useful when overlapping multiple laser projectors to cover a massive stage. This allows you to blend and fade the intersecting laser zones smoothly.

 

See video Soft Edge Blending Guide for more tips.

Laser Scanner (MAD Laser Scanner)

The Laser Scanner is an advanced auto-calibration tool that uses a camera to read test patterns drawn by your physical laser projector. It calculates the exact physical distortion and automatically generates a corrected mapping.

(See the Special Tools and Auto-Calibration page for detailed instructions on using the Laser Scanner).

 

Laser Visualization & Safety

Beam Preview

Under the Preferences > Project > Laser tab, you can customize the internal Beam Preview. It is important to note that this preview does not affect the physical output of your actual laser projector. However, it is an incredibly powerful feature because this simulation can be outputted onto standard video projectors or LED screens if desired, allowing you to seamlessly blend real lasers with simulated ones.

You can adjust the following parameters to customize the simulation:

  • Level: Adjusts the overall brightness and intensity of the simulated beam.

  • Beam Origin: Click and drag the node on the X/Y grid to simulate the physical placement of the laser projector relative to your output surface.

  • End Beam on path: Toggles whether the simulated beam visually stops exactly where it hits the drawing surface or continues passing through.

  • Enable Fog: Activates a volumetric atmospheric effect in the preview window, making it easier to visualize the "mid-air" trajectory of your beams.

  • Reset to Defaults: A quick button to revert all preview settings back to their standard state.

Laser Safety Zones & Masks

When working with high-power lasers, safety must be the primary concern. MadMapper allows you to configure area limitations or "safety masks" within the Laser Output menu. These masks are designed to physically block the software from drawing beams in designated unsafe areas (such as the audience or performer eye-lines).

These masks can be configured under the Outputs tab. Open Outputs, select your laser(s), and scroll down to the mask section associated with your layer. From here, you can create a final output mask that will be applied broadly to anything that passes through that specific projector's placement. If you are using multiple lasers, you may need to configure separate laser masks for each device to ensure that your laser beams are terminating in safe locations and not directly on your audience, live performers and dancers, or dangerous surfaces (flammable, reflective, etc.). When configuring your laser, ensure that these safety masks are created first and enabled so that they remain intact from the beginning to the end of your laser's operation.

However, software masks should never be your only line of defense. Most laser safety experts strongly advise using physical blocks (such as black foil or metal plating attached to the physical projector) to protect crowds and safety zones. A software crash or accidental parameter shift could bypass a digital mask, whereas a physical block guarantees the beam cannot hit a restricted area.

⚠️ CRITICAL LASER SAFETY WARNING ⚠️ High-power lasers can cause permanent eye damage, skin burns, and fire. You are strictly responsible for complying with all local, regional, and national laser safety laws in your country. Always ensure beam paths are safely terminated, use a physical safety switch (e-stop) on your hardware, and never operate without appropriate hardware safety monitoring systems engaged. Use at your own risk.

Previsualization Setup (Capture / Depence / WYSIWYG / LA.preview)

Because laser data is output via Art-Net, you can send your laser paths to 3D simulation software on the same computer to visualize your show before arming the physical hardware.

Before configuring your network, go into MadMapper Preferences > Project > Laser and check the box to enable External Visualisation. Use the dropdown menu to select your specific previsualization software: Capture, Wysiwyg, Depence, or LA.preview.

However, an issue often arises where MadMapper and the visualizer (like Capture) attempt to use the same Art-Net port (6454) on the localhost, causing a conflict. To solve this, you must create a Virtual Network Interface.

Creating a Virtual Network (macOS 14+)

On newer versions of macOS, creating a virtual interface through System Settings prompts you to select a "device interface" to combine physical network cards. Since we want a purely virtual network, you must use Terminal.

Step 1: Create the Virtual Network

  1. Open Terminal (Applications → Utilities → Terminal).
  2. Copy and paste the following command: sudo ifconfig lo0 alias 2.0.0.2 255.0.0.0
  3. Press Enter and type your Mac password (it will remain hidden while typing).
  4. Press Enter again. (Note: This Terminal command is temporary. You will need to run it again after restarting your Mac).

Step 2: Configure MadMapper

  1. Open MadMapper and go to Preferences → Project → DMX Output.
  2. Set Protocol to Art-Net.
  3. Set Network Interface to 2.0.0.2.
  4. Turn OFF Use Unicast (Output Mode must be Broadcast).
  5. Note your Start Universe (e.g., 0).

Step 3: Configure Capture

  1. Open Capture and go to Project → External Universes.
  2. Enable Art-Net.
  3. Set the Universe to 1. (Important: MadMapper's Universe 0 = Capture's Universe 1).
  4. If the connection fails, check your Mac's firewall settings to ensure Capture and MadMapper are allowed to communicate.

Creating a Virtual Network (Windows)

Step 1: Create a Loopback Adapter

  1. Open the Device Manager.
  2. Go to Action > Add legacy hardware.
  3. Select "Install the hardware that I manually select from a list (Advanced)".
  4. Choose Network adapters, then select Microsoft as the manufacturer, and choose Microsoft KM-TEST Loopback Adapter.
  5. Once installed, assign it a static IP address (e.g., 192.168.1.50, subnet mask 255.255.255.0) in your Windows Network Connections settings.

Step 2: Configure MadMapper and Capture

  1. In MadMapper, set your Art-Net output to the static IP address you just created (e.g., 192.168.1.50). Turn OFF Unicast.
  2. In Capture, enable Art-Net and match the universe numbers (Remember: MadMapper's Universe 0 = Capture's Universe 1).
