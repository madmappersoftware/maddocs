Hardware Essentials

MadMapper is designed to be lightweight, flexible, and compatible with a wide range of hardware configurations.

 

Whether you’re working on a small projection mapping project or a large-scale installation, here’s what you need to know about supported systems and recommended setups.

What You Need to Run MadMapper

At its core, all you need to get started with MadMapper is a computer. Also meaning even with the Demo version you can explore the interface, design your mappings, and experiment with ideas entirely on-screen.

 

If you plan to create projection mapping, you'll need a projector. Most modern models will work; brightness, throw distance, and lens options simply depend on your project’s scale. We break down the key considerations below.

 

For light and laser mapping, you’ll find additional recommendations and best practices in the dedicated sections further down.

 

And of course, be prepared with the usual essentials: video adapters, extension cables, power solutions, and other connectors depending on your setup. A well-organized toolkit goes a long way during live installations.

Supported Platforms

MadMapper v6 runs seamlessly on both macOS and Windows, offering the same powerful performance and features across platforms.

macOS: version 10.9 or higher

Windows: version 10 or higher

 

There is no “better” platform, both systems are fully supported and deliver excellent performance.

 

Your choice will mostly come down to personal preference, budget, and the kind of projects you plan to create. Some artists prefer macOS for its streamlined workflow and system stability, while others choose Windows for its flexibility and broader hardware options.

 

Ultimately, both platforms let you get the most out of MadMapper’s creative potential.

macOS Recommendations

MadMapper performs well even on modest Apple hardware, making it an excellent choice for portability and reliability.

  • Entry-level MacBooks (such as MacBook Air or older MacBook Pro models) are perfectly fine for comnon projection mapping tasks.

  • If you plan to work extensively with heavy shaders, multiple video inputs, large 3D content, etc. you’ll benefit from a more powerful GPU (graphics card).

  • Even a second-hand MacBook can be suitable for light to medium use.

MadMapper has been a native M-software for Apple Silicon (M-series) computers since version 5 and above.

Windows Recommendations

MadMapper runs equally well on Windows systems, as long as they’re properly configured.

  • Processor (CPU): Choose a recent, high-performance model (Intel i5/i7/i9 or AMD Ryzen series for instance). Avoid entry-level CPUs, which can bottleneck performance.
  • Graphics Card (GPU): A dedicated GPU is essential. NVIDIA and AMD cards are both supported.
  • Memory (RAM): At least 8 GB is required; 16 GB or more is recommended for smooth performance.
  • Storage: An SSD drive is strongly recommended for faster project loading and video playback.

Even compact PCs such as Intel NUCs or mini PCs can handle projection mapping surprisingly well, it can be ideal for small or portable setups.

Output Considerations

Although you can be limited with you Windows computer too, modern Apple computers often feature limited video outputs, hence the need of additional hardware.

 

To connect multiple projectors or displays, you might need an adapter or splitter, such as:

  • Matrox TripleHead2Go

  • Datapath FX4 (a more advanced and reliable option)

  • StarTech multi-output adapters (affordable and practical)

Think of these devices as “output duplicators” that expand your available outputs.

MadMapper supports unlimited outputs with any license, the only limitation comes from your hardware setup.

Projectors

Which projector should you choose?!

 

That’s the big question, and the answer really depends on your project. The ideal projector for a small indoor installation won’t be the same as the one you’d use to light up the main floor of Berghain, or the façade of the Eiffel Tower.

Keep in mind this three key factors too: projection distance, the size and material of your surface, and the surrounding environment.

So are you working in a dark, controlled space, or outdoors in ambient city light? Do you need portability, or maximum brightness and resolution?

Each of these elements will help define the type of projector that best fits your project.

 

We’ll share below a few projector insights to help you get started.

Image Resolution

A projector’s resolution defines how many pixels it can display, directly influencing the sharpness and clarity of the image.

The higher the resolution, the more detailed and precise the projection becomes.

Choosing a projector with adequate resolution can be especially important for large screens or detailed content.

A minimum of 720p HD is recommended for basic use.

For sharper, more detailed projections, Full HD (1080p) is preferable, and 4K is ideal for the highest level of clarity and precision (think large surfaces or with many details).

Brightness

It’s a crucial factor to consider when selecting a projector!

It’s measured in lumens and reflects the intensity of the light emitted by the device.

As a general rule, a higher number of lumens results in a clearer image that is easier to see (even in brightly lit environments).

Contrast

Contrast is the difference between the light and dark areas of a projected image.

It is usually measured as a ratio: the higher the ratio, the greater the distinction between light and dark areas.

It is important to keep in mind too that absolute black does not really exist - the darkest areas usually appear as a very deep gray.

Refresh Rate

The refresh rate of a projector, measured in Hertz (Hz), indicates how many times per second the image is updated on the screen.

Standard refresh rates are usually 50 Hz or 60 Hz, depending on regional broadcasting norms or device specifications.

A higher refresh rate can result in smoother motion and reduce flickering, which is especially noticeable when projecting fast-moving content.

Ambient Light

Ambient light is fundamental in the overall quality and visibility of a projection.

Excessive surrounding light can wash out colors, reduce contrast, and make images appear faded or unclear.

For optimal results, try to minimize external light sources by darkening the environment as much as possible or by choosing projectors with higher brightness to compensate.

Using Multiple Projectors

You may need to use several projectors at the same time for your mapping. A standard example would be when projecting onto a (very) large surface.

But first and foremost, we do recommended to ensure that your computer has the necessary resources, generally related to the capabilities of the graphics card. Handling such setup and multiple projectors at the same time can be very demanding for entry-level computers!

If your computer does not have enough outputs, you will need to use a dedicated device to duplicate them, see Output Considerations above.

Preventing Dust

Caution! Projectors tend to accumulate dust, which can cause overheating or malfunctions.
Remember to clean the ventilation system regularly to maintain your device's performance and extend its service life.

Transport Safety

When moving or setting up projectors, it’s important to handle them with care to maintain their performance and extend their lifespan.

Always transport the equipment in protective cases, avoid sudden shocks or vibrations, and allow the projector to cool down completely before packing it.

Proper handling and regular precautions help preserve both the optical quality and the internal components over time.

Light Mapping

MadMapper's MadLight native feature dedicated to light mapping seamlessly supports DMX, Art-Net or sACN protocols, allowing you to control LED fixtures, moving heads, and other compatible lighting devices.

DMX Protocol

DMX (short for Digital Multiplex) is the industry-standard protocol used to control lighting equipment such as LED fixtures, dimmers, moving heads, and other stage devices. It was originally developed for theater and live events but has since become a cornerstone of interactive and audiovisual installations. DMX works by sending digital signals, allowing precise control over parameters.

Art-Net, sACN Protocol

These are network-based protocols designed to transmit DMX data over standard Ethernet.

It was created to make large-scale lighting setups more flexible and easier to manage, allowing you to send multiple DMX universes through a single network cable.

Unlike traditional DMX connections, which are limited by cable length and channel count, they let you distribute data efficiently across long distances and complex installations.

Unicast vs Multicast

Unicast sends data directly from one device to another. It uses the unique IP address of each device to deliver specific data (one-to-one).

Multicast sends data to multiple devices at once on the network. It's a method in which devices subscribe to the data they need (one-to-many).

Art-Net is capable of broadcast and unicast, while sACN is capable of unicast and multicast.

Multicast (think sACN) is often preferred for large installations because it reduces network traffic and ensures all devices receive the signal simultaneously!

LED by garageCube

Since its successful Kickstarter launch, LED by garageCube has remained a user-friendly and highly adaptable LED system, designed for both beginners and professionals. Each unit can be assembled easily using sturdy yet lightweight aluminum supports, making it simple to transport, set up, and reconfigure for different projects.

 

The system offers an ideal introduction to the world of DMX lighting, while remaining perfectly suited for scalable, upgradeable installations. You can expand your setup at any time by adding LED bars in different formats (available in RGB or Pure White versions) or even incorporate DMX bulbs.

 

Although LED by garageCube is compatible with any software supporting the DMX protocol, it truly shines when paired with MadMapper. Developed alongside MadMapper’s MadLight feature, the LED system integrates seamlessly with it, allowing you to design and control light with precision, flexibility, and a whole lot of fun.

While this hardware works seamlessly with MadMapper, you are of course free to use any other DMX-compatible equipment that fits your project.

Usb-Dmx Controllers

To enable communication between your computer (and MadMapper) and your DMX hardware, you’ll need a dedicated controller. This device will basically interpret the transmitted signal. It's mandatory!

 

A USB to DMX controller is generally a small plug-and-play box allowing you to control a few universes. For instance, the garageCube one (no driver required to use it with MadMapper) can handle up to 512 DMX channels (= 8 meters of -any- LED by garageCube).

Since MadMapper 4, you can use multiple usb-to-dmx controllers simultaneously by adding multiple DMX Router Modules.

Although we would always recommend an Art-Net Controller for serious installations!

Art-Net Controllers

Art-Net protocol transmits data over Ethernet networks. It’s ideal for large-scale and synchronized lighting projects. Art-Net controllers are much larger and capable devices than a usb-dmx ones.

 

They can handle multiple universes, for example the garageCube one can manage up to 8 DMX universes (= 64 meters of LED by garageCube).

Note that web page admin setup is a standard for such controllers!

This is again the perfect companion for large installations requiring to manage multiple DMX universes.

It's also considered the most reliable option as well as the most versatile one since it allows you to control other DMX devices.

Laser DAC

Since MadMapper 5, you can extend the software's functionality with extensions such as MadLaser.

MadLaser allows you to do laser mapping while benefiting from the MadMapper workflow, including realtime control.

 

You will need a compatible Laser DAC though! DACs being the bridge between the software and the laser hardware.

Compatible DACs are listed below. More to come!

LaserCube

https://www.laseros.com

We support LaserCube Wifi, Ethernet and usb on all platforms.

Although both protocols are supported, prefer Ethernet over Wi-Fi. Please note that the USB version is Mac only.

Ether Dream

https://ether-dream.com

Ether Dream (ethernet - 16 bits XY) needs to run on a clean network without other traffic. For instance, don’t mix Art-Net and Etherdream on the same network or you’ll get disconnections. You can safely put a bunch of Ether Dream on the same network though.

ShowNET

https://www.laserworld.com

MadMapper supports ShowNET protocol - DAC, so any LaserWorld projector with ShowNET will work directly with just a network cable (Auto IP works fine). Also prefer putting your ShowNET devices on a network without other traffic for best smoothness.

Check your laser device documentation to see how to set the ShowNET dipswitch for the network mode you’re using (DHCP / Auto-IP or static IP).

If your ShowNET device appears as a destination in MadMapper Laser Output destinations but does not connect (status keeps “Disconnected”), verify that you don’t have multiple network interfaces on the same IP address range (ie 192.168.0.xxx / 255.255.255.0).

If you have a wifi or another network in the same IP range, either disable your other network adapter or change its IP address, or change the ShowNET pins for “Auto-IP” mode (& your computer interface in DHCP so your system setups an AutoIP address too (169.254.xxx.xxx / 255.255.0.0).

Recommend Laser device for testing

It depends on the budget and possible projects. For graphics (needs accurate scanning, precise and small beam, so low divergence and good diodes alignment) or for atmospheric.

For video projection + laser on top, a small beam (low divergence) may be better. For architectural mapping, atmospheric, clubbing or festival, we would prefer power over sharpness. But for a museum piece for instance it’s better to choose a low power/sharp beam one.

An exemple would be the LaserWorld Tarm 3W : good whites (not need to lower blue by 50%), sharp enough and 3W is ok for atmospheric.

Note that lot of laser devices look close in term of quality. LaserWorld is nice because they have an ethernet DAC inside (ShowNET) so you don't need an external DAC (ethernet or USB -> ILDA).

Safety Recommendations

Lasers are powerful and dangerous, hence they should be handled with care and safety for the audience in mind. So please comply with your local regulations.

Please note that MadMapper cannot be hold responsible for misusage of laser equipment.

Above all, don’t shoot rays towards your audience, always use a safety switch on your hardware and specify a safety area.

Avoid burning your laser hardware by limiting the laser scan rate too.

Other Hardware

What else could you possibly need?


For example, a MIDI controller for your live performances, or a capture card such as a BlackMagic's DeckLink.

 

As for controllers, as long as it supports MIDI, you're good to go. We don't have any particular models or formats to recommend. It all depends on your tastes and budget.

Today's MIDI controllers come in a wide variety of models, with knobs, faders, or more pad-oriented designs, and in all price ranges.
