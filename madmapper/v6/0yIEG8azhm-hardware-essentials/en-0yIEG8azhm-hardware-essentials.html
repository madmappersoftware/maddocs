Hardware Essentials

MadMapper is designed to be lightweight, flexible, and compatible with a wide range of hardware configurations.

 

Whether you’re working on a small projection mapping project or a large-scale installation, here’s what you need to know about supported systems and recommended setups.

What You Need to Run MadMapper

At its core, all you need to get started with MadMapper is a computer. Also meaning even with the Demo version you can explore the interface, design your mappings, and experiment with ideas entirely on-screen.

 

If you plan to create projection mapping, you'll need a projector. Most modern models will work; brightness, throw distance, and lens options simply depend on your project’s scale. We break down the key considerations below.

 

For light and laser mapping, you’ll find additional recommendations and best practices in the dedicated sections further down.

 

And of course, be prepared with the usual essentials: video adapters, extension cables, power solutions, and other connectors depending on your setup. A well-organized toolkit goes a long way during live installations.

Supported Platforms

MadMapper v6 runs seamlessly on both macOS and Windows.

macOS: version 10.9 or higher

Windows: version 10 or higher

 

There is no “better” platform, both systems are fully supported and deliver excellent performance.

The choice usually depends on your personal preference, budget, and the type of projects you plan to create.

macOS Recommendations

MadMapper performs well even on modest Apple hardware, making it an excellent choice for portability and reliability.

  • Entry-level MacBooks (such as MacBook Air or older MacBook Pro models) are perfectly fine for comnon projection mapping tasks.

  • If you plan to work extensively with heavy shaders, multiple video inputs, large 3D content, etc. you’ll benefit from a more powerful GPU (graphics card).

  • Even a second-hand MacBook can be suitable for light to medium use.

MadMapper has been a native M-software for Apple Silicon (M-series) computers since version 5 and above.

Output Considerations (macOS)

Modern MacBooks often feature limited video outputs (USB-C or Thunderbolt).

 

To connect multiple projectors or displays, you might need an adapter or splitter, such as:

  • Matrox TripleHead2Go

  • Datapath FX4 (a more advanced and reliable option)

  • StarTech multi-output adapters (affordable and practical)

Think of these devices as “video multipliers” that expand your available outputs.

MadMapper supports unlimited outputs with any license, the only limitation comes from your hardware setup.

Windows Recommendations

MadMapper runs equally well on Windows systems, as long as they’re properly configured.

  • Processor (CPU): Choose a recent, high-performance model (Intel i5/i7/i9 or AMD Ryzen series). Avoid entry-level CPUs, which can bottleneck performance.
  • Graphics Card (GPU): A dedicated GPU is essential. NVIDIA and AMD cards are both supported.
  • Memory (RAM): At least 8 GB is required; 16 GB or more is recommended for smooth performance.
  • Storage: An SSD drive is strongly recommended for faster project loading and video playback.

Even compact PCs such as Intel NUCs or mini PCs can handle projection mapping surprisingly well, it can be ideal for small or portable setups.

Projectors

Image Resolution

A projector’s resolution defines how many pixels it can display, directly influencing the sharpness and clarity of the image.

The higher the resolution, the more detailed and precise the projection becomes.

Choosing a projector with adequate resolution can be especially important for large screens or detailed content.

A minimum of 720p HD is recommended for basic use.

For sharper, more detailed projections, Full HD (1080p) is preferable, and 4K is ideal for the highest level of clarity and precision (think large surfaces or with many details).

Brightness

It’s a crucial factor to consider when selecting a projector!

It’s measured in lumens and reflects the intensity of the light emitted by the device.

As a general rule, a higher number of lumens results in a clearer image that is easier to see (even in brightly lit environments).

Contrast

Contrast is the difference between the light and dark areas of a projected image.

It is usually measured as a ratio: the higher the ratio, the greater the distinction between light and dark areas.

It is important to keep in mind too that absolute black does not really exist - the darkest areas usually appear as a very deep gray.

Refresh Rate

The refresh rate of a projector, measured in Hertz (Hz), indicates how many times per second the image is updated on the screen.

Standard refresh rates are usually 50 Hz or 60 Hz, depending on regional broadcasting norms or device specifications.

A higher refresh rate can result in smoother motion and reduce flickering, which is especially noticeable when projecting fast-moving content.

Ambient Light

Ambient light is fundamental in the overall quality and visibility of a projection.

Excessive surrounding light can wash out colors, reduce contrast, and make images appear faded or unclear.

For optimal results, try to minimize external light sources by darkening the environment as much as possible or by choosing projectors with higher brightness to compensate.

Using Multiple Projectors

You may need to use several projectors at the same time for your mapping. A standard example would be when projecting onto a (very) large surface.

But first and foremost, we do recommended to ensure that your computer has the necessary resources, generally related to the capabilities of the graphics card. Handling such setup and multiple projectors at the same time can be very demanding for entry-level computers!

If your computer does not have enough outputs, you will need to use a dedicated device to duplicate them, see Output Considerations above.

Preventing Dust

Caution! Projectors tend to accumulate dust, which can cause overheating or malfunctions.
Remember to clean the ventilation system regularly to maintain your device's performance and extend its service life.

Transport Safety

When moving or setting up projectors, it’s important to handle them with care to maintain their performance and extend their lifespan.

Always transport the equipment in protective cases, avoid sudden shocks or vibrations, and allow the projector to cool down completely before packing it.

Proper handling and regular precautions help preserve both the optical quality and the internal components over time.
