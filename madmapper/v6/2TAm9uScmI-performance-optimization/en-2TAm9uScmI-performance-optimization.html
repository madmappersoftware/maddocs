Performance Optimization (Large Installations)

When pushing millions of pixels to multiple outputs, balancing your CPU, GPU, and internal storage bandwidth is critical.

GPU Tuning & The Frame Tearing Problem

  • macOS: Modern Apple Silicon (M-Series) machines utilize unified memory, making them exceptionally efficient for large-scale video processing, especially when utilizing native ProRes hardware acceleration to bypass CPU bottlenecks.

  • Windows (PC): A dedicated, high-end NVIDIA GPU is heavily recommended. However, simply plugging four projectors into the four ports of a standard consumer NVIDIA RTX card will almost always result in frame tearing. Because consumer cards cannot perfectly synchronize the refresh cycles of multiple independent display heads, fast-moving horizontal visuals will look fractured across your projectors.

  • The Media Server Solution: To achieve true, genlocked frame synchronization across multiple GPU outputs on a PC, you must use a professional-grade NVIDIA Quadro card paired with a dedicated Quadro Sync II card to lock the outputs to a master timing signal.

The Hardware Splitter Strategy

To guarantee perfect frame sync without building a highly complex Quadro Sync media server, the industry-standard approach is to utilize external display controllers.

  • How it Works: You drive a single, massive 4K or 8K output from your computer's GPU. Because the computer only sees one physical display, frame tearing is impossible. That single high-resolution signal is then routed into a hardware splitter, which physically divides the raster into multiple independent 1080p or 4K feeds for your projectors.

  • Industry Standards: Devices like the Datapath Fx4 (or Fx4-HDR) can take a single 4K60 signal over DisplayPort and flawlessly slice it into four synchronized HD outputs. Other reliable alternatives include the Matrox QuadHead2Go, or utilizing PCIe capture/playback cards like the Blackmagic DeckLink 8K Pro configured for quad-split output.

Storage Bandwidth & The HAP Codec

For large installations, relying on highly compressed delivery formats (like H.264 or HEVC) will instantly bottleneck your CPU as it struggles to decode the math in real-time, resulting in stuttering playback.

  • The HAP Strategy: You must render your installation media using the HAP codec family (HAP for standard playback, HAP Alpha for transparency, or HAP Q for higher visual fidelity). HAP bypasses the CPU entirely and sends the video decompression workload straight to the GPU.

  • The NVMe Bottleneck: The trade-off for zero CPU load is massive file sizes. Standard 5400RPM or 7200RPM spinning hard drives cannot read data fast enough to play multiple HAP files. You must use high-speed internal PCIe NVMe M.2 SSDs (capable of 3,000 to 7,000 MB/s read speeds) or high-bandwidth Thunderbolt RAID arrays.

  • Benchmarking: Always benchmark your storage drives using tools like the Blackmagic Disk Speed Test. You must mathematically ensure that the sustained read speed of your drive heavily exceeds the combined total megabytes-per-second of all the video files playing in your timeline simultaneously.

Network Bandwidth & NDI Stream Management

If you are routing live generative video between computers (e.g., from a dedicated TouchDesigner PC into a MadMapper Mac) using NDI, you must carefully manage your network topology.

  • Bandwidth Math: A single 1080p 60fps NDI High Bandwidth stream consumes roughly 125 to 150 Mbps of network data.

  • Gigabit Limitations: Sending multiple uncompressed NDI streams will quickly saturate a standard 1-Gigabit (1,000 Mbps) network switch. Network engineers strongly recommend leaving 25% to 30% of your network as "headroom" to prevent packet loss. If your NDI traffic exceeds 750 Mbps, your streams will begin to stutter and drop frames.

  • 10GbE Infrastructure: For large-scale installations requiring multiple 4K NDI feeds, upgrading your infrastructure to a 10-Gigabit (10GbE) managed network switch and utilizing Cat6a ethernet cabling is strictly required to ensure stable, zero-latency transmission.
