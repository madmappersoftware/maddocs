Glossary of Terms

A quick reference guide for common terminology used in projection mapping, live audiovisual performance, and stage lighting.

 

A

  • Alpha Channel: The portion of a digital image or video file that determines its transparency, allowing background layers to show through.

  • Ambient Color: In 3D lighting, the baseline illumination applied to the shadows of a calibrated 3D object.

  • Art-Net: A network protocol used to transmit DMX512 lighting data over standard Ethernet cables, essential for controlling large modular LED installations without relying on heavy DMX cables.

  • Attenuation: The rate at which digital light falls off or decays over a distance in a 3D scene (e.g., Quadratic falloff).

  • Audio Routing Matrix: A tool within MadMapper used to assign multi-channel audio inputs and outputs to specific sound cards or software protocols (like BlackHole or JACK).

  • Auto-Calibration: Using camera-based computer vision to automatically calculate the relationship between a projector’s lens and a physical surface, generating a precise map.

  • AVB / Dante: Audio Video Bridging and Dante are network protocols for sending high-fidelity, low-latency audio across a local network.

B

  • Beat Detection: An audio analysis tool that listens to an incoming sound source and automatically generates triggers synchronized to the tempo.

  • Bezier Curve: A mathematical curve defined by control points and handles, used in MadMapper for smooth surface warping and complex masking around organic architectural shapes.

  • Bezier Handle (Tangent): An adjustable control point that dictates the arc and trajectory of a Bezier curve.

  • Blanking: In laser projection, the rapid turning off of the laser diode as the mirrors move from one shape to another, preventing unwanted "tail" lines between separate graphics.

  • Blend Mode: The mathematical calculation (like Additive, Multiply, or Screen) determining how a top layer interacts with the colors and light of the layers beneath it.

  • BPM (Beats Per Minute): The master tempo clock that drives audio-reactive animations, timelines, and automated light pulses.

C

  • Calibration Map: A high-resolution, pixel-perfect map generated by spatial scanners to guide projection mapping workflows without remaining in the final output.

  • Circle Surface: A geometric video surface defined by a central scale point and four outer handles, ideal for mapping round architectural features.

  • Conductor: MadMapper’s grid-based cueing system, allowing operators to trigger coordinated scenes, lighting changes, and audio tracks simultaneously during a live show.

  • Coordinate-based Mapping: Mapping surfaces using precise X, Y, and Z spatial coordinates rather than just relative 2D pixel positions.

  • Corner Pinning: A foundational mapping technique that warps a surface by manipulating its four corner points to mimic the projector’s 3D perspective.

  • Crop: The act of isolating a specific region of a source media file using the Input Rectangle.

D

  • DAC (Digital-to-Analog Converter): The hardware interface that translates digital vector data from a computer into the analog ILDA signals required by a laser projector.

  • Dimmer: The DMX channel specifically assigned to control the overall brightness or intensity of a lighting fixture.

  • DMX512: The industry-standard digital communication network used to control stage lighting and effects.

  • DMX Addressing: The process of assigning a unique starting channel number to a physical lighting fixture so the software can communicate with it independently.

  • DMX Node: A hardware device that translates network-based DMX (Art-Net/sACN) into physical 5-pin or 3-pin DMX cables.

E

  • EDID (Extended Display Identification Data): The data signal a monitor or projector sends to a computer's GPU to communicate its supported resolutions and refresh rates.

  • Edge Blending: The technique of overlapping the edges of multiple projectors and applying a luminance gradient to hide the seams, creating a seamless, panoramic image.

  • EtherDream: A popular brand of laser DAC supported natively by MadLaser.

  • Exposure: A setting in 3D lighting that dictates the overall brightness and intensity of a digital spotlight.

F

  • Feathering: A parameter used to soften the hard edges of a mask or surface, allowing it to blend smoothly into the physical background.

  • Firmata: A generic protocol for communicating with microcontrollers (like Arduino) from software on a host computer.

  • Fixture Profile (Definition): A digital file that tells the software how a specific DMX fixture works, mapping channels to functions like Red, Green, Blue, Dimmer, or Strobe.

  • FreeDraw Tool: A MadMapper tool allowing users to click and drag to organically draw paths, which are instantly converted to smooth Bezier lines.

  • Full Screen Mode: The command to dispatch the MadMapper output directly to the connected projector or secondary display.

G

  • Generative Light: Lighting or video patterns created in real-time by algorithms or code (GLSL) rather than pre-rendered video files, allowing for infinite variations.

  • GLSL (OpenGL Shading Language): The code language used to write custom generative materials and shaders.

  • Grid Generator: A built-in MadMapper test pattern used as a visual aid to align architectural features during mesh warping.

  • Grouping: Organizing multiple surfaces into a single folder, allowing the operator to scale, move, or apply opacity changes to the entire collection at once.

H

  • Helios USB: A compact, low-cost laser DAC supported natively by MadLaser.

  • High-Density Pixel Matrix: A large-scale arrangement of thousands of individual LED pixels, often configured using the Video Wall Template.

I

  • ICS (Internet Calendar Scheduling): A master setting feature that uses standard calendar files to automate the triggering of projects, system power, or lighting states at specific dates and times.

  • ILDA: The International Laser Display Association standard; refers to both the analog DB25 connection type and the signal protocol used to transmit vector imagery.

  • Input Mesh: The grid on the source media side; linking the input mesh to the output mesh ensures that physical warping applied to the projector output does not distort the source video content.

  • Input Rectangle: The specific boundary hovering over a source media file that dictates exactly which part of the video will be sampled for the mapped surface.

  • Inspector (Surface Inspector): The UI panel that displays all adjustable parameters (media, FX, warping, opacity) for the currently selected surface.

L

  • Laser Dispatch: A MadLaser feature that intelligently spreads complex, high-point-count laser content across multiple laser projectors to maintain smooth frame rates and prevent flickering.

  • Laser Safety Zone: A defined physical area where the laser beam is blocked or intensity-dimmed to ensure audience and camera safety, configured using Bezier masks.

  • Laser Scanner: An auto-calibration tool that utilizes the laser projector itself to trace and map the physical environment.

  • LED Scanner: An auto-calibration tool that uses a camera to map individual DMX pixels in physical space by flashing them sequentially.

  • Line Surface (madLine): A versatile surface type designed for freehand drawing, interactive line animation, and ultra-precise vector paths for laser projection.

  • Loopback: A technique where output data (like audio or video) is routed back into the same computer as an input source.

M

  • MadAI: A MadMapper extension that allows operators to generate custom 2D FX, materials, and shaders using simple text-based prompts.

  • MadLaser: The official extension that enables real-time vectorization of visuals and output to laser projectors using native mapping workflows.

  • MadMaterial: A proprietary format for generative GLSL shaders that can be assigned directly to surfaces as video content.

  • MadProjectorControl: An advanced module that interfaces with projectors over a network to automate power states, shutter control, and input switching.

  • Masking: The use of alpha shapes to hide specific parts of a composition from the projector, ensuring light doesn't spill onto unwanted areas.

  • Media Bin: The UI section where all imported videos, images, generators, and 3D objects are stored and managed.

  • Mesh Warping: An advanced geometry technique applying an internal grid of points to a surface, allowing it to be deformed to fit complex curves.

  • MIDI (Musical Instrument Digital Interface): A standard protocol used to connect electronic musical instruments and controllers to MadMapper to trigger cues and manipulate faders.

  • MiniMad: A dedicated hardware companion device designed for the standalone playback of exported MadMapper projects.

N

  • NDI (Network Device Interface): A high-performance protocol used to send high-quality video over an IP network, enabling zero-latency video sharing between different computers and media servers.

O

  • .OBJ File: A standard 3D model format that can be imported into MadMapper for 3D calibration, wireframe rendering, and digital lighting.

  • OSC (Open Sound Control): A modern, high-resolution network protocol for triggering and controlling software parameters; often superior to MIDI for complex live AV setups.

  • Output Perspective: The background calculation that automatically corrects 3D perspective distortion when moving the corners of a Quad surface.

  • Output Window: The final rendered canvas that is sent from the computer's graphics card to the physical projector or LED processor.

P

  • Perspective Pinning: A technique that warps a surface by manipulating its corner points to mimic the projector’s 3D perspective, preventing visual skewing.

  • Pixel Mapping: The act of translating a 2D video canvas into specific data channels to drive physical LED modules, tubes, or fixtures.

  • PJLink: A standardized network protocol used to remotely control projector power, input sources, and status monitoring across different projector brands.

  • Point Cloud: A collection of data points defining the geometry of a laser shape; lasers rapidly trace these points to create persistent images.

  • Projection Mapping: The technique of using specialized software to warp and blend projected video onto complex physical geometry, turning everyday objects into dynamic displays.

Q

  • Quad (Video Quad): A four-sided polygon surface. The foundational building block for mapping flat rectangular surfaces like screens, windows, or building facades.

R

  • RDM (Remote Device Management): A protocol enhancement to DMX512 that allows bi-directional communication, meaning the software can configure and monitor fixtures remotely.

  • RGB / RGBW: Color mixing standards for LED fixtures representing Red, Green, Blue, and White channels.

  • RMS Error (Root Mean Square): A mathematical metric displayed during 3D calibration that indicates how accurately your virtual points match the physical target.

S

  • sACN (Streaming ACN): A modern, multicast network protocol for transmitting DMX data over Ethernet, favored for large-scale and architectural lighting installations.

  • Shadow Group: An organizational folder where masks and blend modes are isolated, ensuring effects within the group do not negatively interact with the rest of the workspace.

  • ShowNET: A popular internal laser mainboard and DAC protocol supported directly by MadLaser.

  • Soft-Edge Blending: The use of precise gradients to blend the output of two or more overlapping projectors into a single, seamless image.

  • Spatial Scanner: A tool that projects structured light patterns captured by a camera to automatically calibrate a projector's perspective relative to the physical scene.

  • Spout / Syphon: Frameworks (Spout for Windows, Syphon for macOS) used to share real-time video frames between different applications running on the same computer with zero latency.

  • Stick Fonts: Single-line fonts highly optimized for laser projection, allowing for high-speed text rendering that avoids the flickering associated with standard filled fonts.

  • Structured Light Patterns: The black-and-white grid patterns projected by the Spatial Scanner to calculate physical depth and geometry.

  • Subdivisions: The individual rows and columns generated to create a mesh grid for advanced warping.

  • Surface: A virtual digital canvas (Quad, Triangle, Line, Mask, 3D Model) within the software that holds media and receives output parameters.

  • SVG Import: The ability to import Scalable Vector Graphics files, which MadMapper automatically translates into native, mathematically perfect line surfaces.

T

  • Tangent (Bezier): The invisible handle attached to a Bezier point that dictates the trajectory and steepness of the curve.

  • Test Pattern: An inherent visual grid used to verify projector focus, color accuracy, and alignment before applying media.

  • Timeline: A linear playback module used for sequencing video, DMX lighting cues, and laser actions along a time-based track.

  • Triangle Surface: A three-sided geometric surface, highly effective for slicing up complex, non-rectangular objects where quads would overlap awkwardly.

U

  • Universe: A single DMX signal stream containing 512 addressable channels.

  • Unlink Tangents: A command that breaks the continuity of a Bezier handle, allowing the operator to create sharp points and sudden architectural angles rather than smooth curves.

V

  • Vector Graphics: Images defined by mathematical paths (lines and curves) rather than pixels, which is exactly how laser projectors draw content.

  • Video Wall Template: An automated setup tool that rapidly generates a perfectly aligned, scaled grid of video quads or DMX fixtures based on physical panel counts and bezel sizes.

W

  • Warping Point: An anchor point on a mesh grid that can be selected, moved, or manipulated to physically deform the shape of a surface.

  • Wireframe: The raw structural lines of a 3D model (.OBJ); MadMapper and MadLaser can render just the wireframe to create stunning, transparent geometric illusions on physical objects.
