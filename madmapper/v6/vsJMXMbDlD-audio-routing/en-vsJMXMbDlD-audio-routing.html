Audio Routing & Multi-Channel Sound Cards

Advanced installations often require complex audio setups, such as routing audio from MadMapper into a Digital Audio Workstation (DAW) like Ableton Live, or sending discrete multi-channel audio tracks from a single video file to an array of physical speakers. This section details how to configure multi-channel sound cards and virtual audio cables on both macOS and Windows systems.

Multi-Channel Video Playback

If you are playing a movie file encoded with multi-channel audio (e.g., 4.0, 5.1, or 8-channel audio), MadMapper can output all discrete channels simultaneously, provided your operating system and sound card are configured correctly.

  • macOS Configuration: macOS handles multi-channel audio routing natively. Simply open MadMapper, navigate to Preferences > Audio > Audio Output, and select your multi-channel hardware interface.
  • Windows Configuration: By default, standard Windows audio drivers (like Direct Sound) may only provide a stereo output. To enable true multi-channel support on a Windows machine, you must navigate to MadMapper's Preferences > Audio and select the ASIO driver type. (Note: ASIO drivers require exclusive access to the audio device. Ensure no other applications or Windows system sounds are currently using the sound card, or MadMapper will fail to open the driver).

macOS: Virtual Routing with BlackHole

BlackHole is a modern, zero-latency virtual audio driver for macOS that replaces older tools like Soundflower. It acts as an invisible cable, allowing you to pass audio seamlessly from MadMapper into other software.

  • Install BlackHole: Download and install BlackHole (the 16-channel version is highly recommended to ensure you have enough bandwidth for multi-channel video files).
  • Create a Multi-Output Device (Optional but Recommended): If you route MadMapper directly to BlackHole, you will not be able to hear the audio out of your physical speakers. To solve this, open the native macOS Audio MIDI Setup application. Click the + button at the bottom left and select Create Multi-Output Device. Check the boxes for both your physical sound card (e.g., External Headphones or Focusrite) and BlackHole.

  • Route MadMapper Output: In MadMapper's Preferences > Audio, set the Audio Output to either BlackHole or your newly created Multi-Output Device.

  • Receive Audio in DAW: Open your receiving software (like Ableton Live or Logic Pro), go to its audio preferences, and set the Input Device to BlackHole. You can now arm a track to receive and record audio directly from MadMapper.

Windows: Virtual Routing with JACK

The JACK Audio Connection Kit is an industry-standard, professional sound server daemon that allows you to connect a myriad of different audio applications to an audio device, and to route audio between them.

  1. Install JACK: Download the JACK installer for Windows. During the installation process, ensure that you check the box to register JackRouter. This is the crucial virtual ASIO driver that MadMapper will use to communicate with the JACK server.
  2. Start the Server: Open QjackCtl (the JACK Control panel) and click Start. The server must be actively running before you launch your audio applications.
  3. Configure MadMapper: Open MadMapper, navigate to Preferences > Audio, and select the ASIO driver type. Choose JackRouter from the dropdown list.
  4. Configure Receiving Software: Open your target software (e.g., Ableton Live or OBS) and similarly set its input driver to ASIO and device to JackRouter.
  5. Patch the Cables: Return to QjackCtl and click on Graph or Connections. This interface displays all running JackRouter applications. Simply click and drag from MadMapper's output nodes to the input nodes of your desired software or physical multi-channel sound card to establish the virtual connections.

Tip: Ensure that the sample rates (e.g., 44.1kHz or 48kHz) perfectly match across your operating system, MadMapper, your virtual router, and your physical sound card. Mismatched sample rates are the most common cause of clicking, popping, or completely dropped audio streams during a show.
