Video Surfaces

Basics

Video Surfaces allow you to create virtual surfaces for your real world structure.

The different types of shapes, their flexibility of use and their parameters will allow you to handle your simplest or most complex projects with ease.

  • Lock / Unlock a surface.

FreeDraw & Lines

The Line surface (traditionally known as madLine) is one of MadMapper’s most versatile mapping tools. Designed for freehand drawing and interactive line animation, it allows you to trace complex architectural elements, map continuous pixel-LED strips (such as those encased in custom aluminum channels), and generate ultra-precise vector paths for laser projection.

 

Creating a Line Surface

There are two primary workflows for drawing a line surface in the output view:

1. The FreeDraw Method (Organic Curves) Click the Line icon in the surface creation toolbar to add a new surface. Left-click and hold your mouse, then simply drag your cursor across the preview window to draw a continuous freehand shape. When you release the mouse, MadMapper automatically processes your freehand stroke and converts it into a clean, smooth line populated with editable Bezier adjustment points.

2. The Point-by-Point Method (Geometric Lines) Select the Line tool, but instead of dragging, click once to drop your first anchor point. Move your cursor to the next location and click again to drop a second point, continuing this process to build your path. This method is perfect for creating sharp, straight lines, snapping to grids, or tracing asymmetrical, modular stage designs.

Note: To finish your line and exit drawing mode, press Enter or Esc on your keyboard.

Bezier Adjustments & Editing

Once your line is drawn, it is not permanently locked. It is fully editable within the workspace:

  • Bezier Handles: By selecting any point along your line, you can expose its Bezier handles. Clicking and dragging these handles allows you to bow and curve the line, perfectly contouring your mapping to arched windows, domes, or circular objects.

  • Line Inspector Properties: In the Surface Inspector, you can deeply customize the physical properties of the line. You can adjust the overall line thickness, modify the join styles (how sharp corners connect and bevel), and adjust how the assigned media texture aligns and stretches along the stroke of the path.

To convert a standard line point to a bezier, simply right-click on the point and select Enable Bezier or press the B key while the point is selected.

SVG Import & Animation

Instead of drawing complex geometry by hand, MadMapper allows you to import vector files. By dropping an .SVG file into your project, MadMapper will instantly translate the vector paths into native Line surfaces.

Because these are mathematically perfect paths, you can assign Materials to them and use MadMapper's internal BPM clock to sync animations—allowing a pulse of light to perfectly trace the perimeter of your SVG design to the beat of the music.

FX (Effects)

The FX section allows you to apply different visual effects directly to your surface. The interface consists of an FX Type toggle to enable or disable the effect, followed by an FX dropdown menu to select the specific effect you want to use. The inspector below it automatically updates to display the adjustable values for your chosen FX.

By default, this is set to Color Controls, which allows you to adjust the media's basic color settings:

 

However, Color Controls are just one type of effect! MadMapper offers a massive library of publicly available 2D FX that can be downloaded and added directly to your layer. Furthermore, because these effects are built using the same MadMaterials GLSL code architecture, you can use MadAI to generate completely new, custom 2D FX using just text prompts—without writing a single line of code!

Advanced 3D & Scanning

3D Calibration

For advanced projection mapping on complex, non-flat physical objects (like cars, statues, or stage sets), you can use the 3D Surface.

  • Importing: Drag and drop an .OBJ file representing your physical object into MadMapper (or select it from the 3D Model dropdown at the top of the Inspector).

  • Matching Perspective: Enable the Calibrate toggle in the Inspector to align the virtual 3D model with the real-world object. Using MadMapper's 3D calibration tool, you select known anchor points on the physical object (e.g., the corners of a stage) and pair them with the exact corresponding points on your 3D model.

  • Error Feedback & Locking: As you plot these calibration points, MadMapper provides real-time RMS error feedback. Once your virtual camera accurately matches the physical projector's lens, click Fix Position to lock the geometry in place. If you need to start over, simply click Reset Calibration.

3D Lighting

Once a 3D surface is calibrated, you are no longer just projecting flat video; you are interacting with physical geometry.

MadMapper's 3D Lighting engine allows you to place virtual digital lights inside your scene. These digital lights will cast realistic, adjustable shadows dynamically across your physical 3D object, completely altering the perceived depth and geometry of the real-world surface without requiring pre-rendered 3D software.

When you enable the Lighting toggle in the Inspector, you gain access to a deep suite of digital illumination tools:

  • Positioning: Adjust the Distance, Longitude, and Latitude sliders to orbit the light source exactly where you need it around your 3D model.

  • Colors: Define the primary Light Color and the base Ambient Col (the baseline illumination in the shadows). You can toggle Light in Preview to ensure you can see these lighting effects directly in your workspace preview.

  • Attenuation: Enable the Attenuated toggle to dictate how light falls off over a distance. You can select the falloff Type (such as Quadratic) to mimic realistic physical light decay.

  • Spotlight: Enable the Spot toggle to turn the omnidirectional light into a focused beam. From here, you can adjust the Exposure and dial in the exact Cutoff angle of the spotlight cone.

  • Shadows: Use the Shadow dropdown to enable and define the resolution/quality of the shadows cast across the model's geometry.

Space Scanner

(Formerly the Spatial Scanner)

If you do not have a pre-built 3D model of your stage or building, you can use the Space Scanner to accurately calibrate your projector's perspective relative to your physical scene.

Understanding the Process

The Space Scanner does not create a 3D model. Instead, it projects a series of structured light patterns (black and white stripes) from your projector, which are captured by your camera. By analyzing how these patterns distort across your physical surfaces, MadMapper calculates the exact perspective of the projector's lens. The result is a high-resolution, pixel-perfect "calibration map" that you can use as a background layer while you work on your mappings.

Note: These background calibration layers are used to guide your workflow and do not have to appear in your final output.

Setup & Configuration

  1. Open the Scanner: Access the tool via Tools > Scanners > Space Scanner.

  2. Select Hardware: In the top toolbar, select your Source (webcam, USB camera, or supported Canon DSLR) and the Projector output.

  3. Adjust Timing: Set your Capture Timing to ensure the camera shutter is synchronized with the projected patterns.

  4. Execute Capture: Click Capture to begin the automated sequence.

Best Practices for Accuracy

To ensure the most accurate calibration, please follow these guidelines:

  • Lens Alignment: Place your camera lens as close to the projector's lens as possible.

  • Stability: Once the process begins, do not move the camera or the projector.

  • Clearance: Ensure no people or objects move in front of the projection during the scan, as any movement will skew the final image calculation.

(See the Special Tools and Auto-Calibration page for detailed instructions on using the LED, Space, or Laser Scanners.

 

Video Wall Setup

When working with massive LED walls powered by external processors (like Novastar) or configuring high-density pixel matrices, building the grid piece-by-piece is inefficient. MadMapper features an automated Video Wall template setup tool designed specifically to streamline this process.

Accessing the Tool

To begin your configuration, navigate to the top menu and select: File > New Project > From Template > Video Wall Template.

Configuration Parameters

Once the setup window appears, define your wall using the following settings:

  • Number of screens: Define the grid layout by entering the horizontal and vertical screen count.

  • Screen resolution: Select a standard resolution (e.g., 1080p) or define a custom pixel width and height.

  • Setup bezel: Enable this if you need to account for physical gaps between your panels, then specify the Image size and Bezel size in cm or inches.

  • Projector orientation: Choose the physical rotation of your screens (e.g., Landscape 0°) to ensure the video canvas aligns correctly.

Once configured, click Create. MadMapper will instantly generate a perfectly aligned, scaled grid of video quads or DMX fixtures, guaranteeing your virtual canvas matches your physical LED panels flawlessly while saving hours of manual configuration.
