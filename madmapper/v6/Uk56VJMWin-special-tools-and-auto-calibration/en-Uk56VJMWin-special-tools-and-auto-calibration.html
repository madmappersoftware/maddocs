Special Tools & Auto-Calibration

When dealing with highly complex stages, scattered LED installations, or intricate architectural mappings, manual calibration can become incredibly time-consuming. MadMapper provides three powerful auto-calibration tools to speed up these advanced workflows.

These features can be accessed from the top menu bar under Tools -> Scanners: Space Scanner, LED Scanner, and Laser Scanner.

Space Scanner

(Formerly the Spatial Scanner)

If you do not have a pre-built 3D model of your stage or building, you can use the Space Scanner to accurately calibrate your projector's perspective relative to your physical scene.

Understanding the Process

The Space Scanner does not create a 3D model. Instead, it projects a series of structured light patterns (black and white stripes) from your projector, which are captured by your camera. By analyzing how these patterns distort across your physical surfaces, MadMapper calculates the exact perspective of the projector's lens. The result is a high-resolution, pixel-perfect "calibration map" that you can use as a background layer while you work on your mappings.

Note: These background calibration layers are used to guide your workflow and do not have to appear in your final output.

Setup & Configuration

  1. Open the Scanner: Access the tool via Tools > Scanners > Space Scanner.

  2. Select Hardware: In the top toolbar, select your Source (webcam, USB camera, or supported Canon DSLR) and the Projector output.

  3. Adjust Timing: Set your Capture Timing to ensure the camera shutter is synchronized with the projected patterns.

  4. Execute Capture: Click Capture to begin the automated sequence.

Best Practices for Accuracy

To ensure the most accurate calibration, please follow these guidelines:

  • Lens Alignment: Place your camera lens as close to the projector's lens as possible.

  • Stability: Once the process begins, do not move the camera or the projector.

  • Clearance: Ensure no people or objects move in front of the projection during the scan, as any movement will skew the final image calculation.

 

LED Scanner

When building custom LED installations, placing physical light tubes or pixel tape rarely results in a perfect geometric grid. Trying to manually arrange dozens of virtual DMX fixtures in MadMapper to match their scattered real-world positions is notoriously difficult. The LED Scanner automates this entirely.

Setup & Configuration

  1. Position the Camera: Position a camera (webcam or DSLR) so it has a clear view of your entire physical LED installation.

  2. Open the Scanner: Access the LED Scanner via Tools > Scanners > LED Scanner.

  3. Configure Parameters: In the LED Scanner panel, define the following settings:

    • Camera: Select the input device (e.g., FaceTime HD Camera).

    • Fixture Definition: Choose the specific profile (e.g., GarageCube - SFX-01 RGB 1m).

    • DMX Range: Define the Start Universe/Channel and End Universe/Channel that the scanner should monitor for your LED installation.

    • Brightness & Threshold: Adjust these sliders to ensure the camera can clearly distinguish the flashing LEDs from the ambient light in the room.

    • Map LEDs at 0,0 if not detected: An optional safety check to catch any pixels that were missed during the scan.

  4. The Scan: Click Start Scan. MadMapper will sequentially flash every single LED pixel in your physical installation one by one.

  5. The Result: The camera tracks these flashes in real-time. Once finished, MadMapper automatically arranges all of your virtual DMX fixtures in your MadLight workspace to perfectly mirror their exact physical coordinates in the real world. You can immediately drop a video texture across your workspace, and it will map flawlessly across your physical LEDs.

 

Laser Scanner

Introduced in MadMapper 5.5, the Laser Scanner brings the power of auto-calibration to the MadLaser extension.

Because laser hardware utilizes high-speed mechanical galvanometers (mirrors) rather than pixel grids, aligning laser paths to physical architecture or syncing them perfectly over a video projection traditionally requires intensive, manual Bezier warping.

By utilizing the Laser Scanner, a connected camera reads calibration patterns drawn by your physical laser projector. The software calculates the exact physical distortion, angle, and distance of the laser beam, and automatically generates a corrected mapping. This ensures that your vector paths, ILDA files, and laser materials hit your physical targets with absolute precision.

Using the Laser Scanner

To use the Laser Scanner, you must first have your laser configured properly. If the laser is armed and capable of outputting an image from MadMapper, you can enable the Laser Scanner, which will create a 16x16 matrix of laser dots.

Similar to the Space Scanner, the Laser Scanner will capture images as the rows and columns of dots move. It is crucial that the camera and laser projector do not move during this process. When completed, the Laser Scanner tool will interpret the information and create a custom background image. You will be asked to choose a name and destination for this image, and then it will be automatically displayed in your laser canvas.

 

The image generated will not appear on the final laser output.
