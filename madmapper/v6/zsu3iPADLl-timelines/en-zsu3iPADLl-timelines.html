Timelines

Timelines Overview & The Conductor

MadMapper 6 introduces a monumental shift in how you program and execute shows: the native Timeline system. Previously, complex projects relied entirely on state-based logic, where you triggered isolated snapshots and let the software crossfade between them. With the introduction of Timelines, MadMapper steps into the role of a centralized, non-linear show control system.

The Conductor acts as your Master Timeline. This is where you can build out a linear sequence that controls almost all aspects of your project simultaneously. Within the Conductor, you can create interactive flags (Markers) to pause, loop, or jump through the sequence. You can drop entire Scenes and Cues directly into a Timeline Player track, while simultaneously controlling non-linear montage tracks, routing video and audio sources, driving geometric surfaces, firing lasers, running DMX lighting sequences, and outputting MIDI/OSC commands to external systems.

Use the shortcut Cmd + Shift + C (Mac) or Ctrl + Shift + C (Windows) to instantly access Edit Mode for The Scenes and Cues.

Scenes & Cues

Before diving into timeline animation, it is important to understand MadMapper's foundational state-recall system.

  • Scenes (Global Snapshots): A Scene acts as an absolute master snapshot of your entire project, recording the exact state of every surface, fixture, and media file.

  • Cues (Granular States): Cues offer surgical precision, allowing you to store and recall only a specific selection of parameters (e.g., just the geometry of one quad) without affecting the rest of your live output.

  • Quick Fade Transitions: Underneath the name of each Scene and Cue in the grid, you can double-click on the time (default 0s) to quickly change the fade transition duration.

Operator Note: While Scenes are great, Cues are extremely powerful. They allow you to make dynamic changes to a live show or project, while keeping your main media playing continuously. While it is possible to modify a Scene to keep playing media when triggered, by default, a scene will store all aspects of your project, which can lead to media restarting, or unexpected behavior while fading between Scenes. Get to know Cues and they'll be a great tool.

Editing & Updating Scenes and Cues

 

If you tweak a Scene or Cue during a show, your live changes are not saved automatically. When editing parameters, keep an eye on the interface highlights: Red highlights indicate that a parameter is currently editable, and a red line means it has been edited. Orange indicates that a parameter is currently selected (or, in batch updating, that the current values differ from the other selected Scenes and Cues).

MadMapper provides three distinct workflows to save your adjustments:

  • The Record Button: Select the Scene or Cue, click the Record button, and edit your values as desired. Click the Record button again to stop editing and save your changes.

    • Batch Updating: If you want to update values across multiple scenes simultaneously, select all the desired scenes, click Record, make your changes, and click Record again to save those new values to every selected item.

  • The Right-Click Method: Make your live adjustments, then right-click the specific Scene or Cue in the grid and select Update Scene (or Update Cue) to overwrite it with your current output.

  • Drag & Drop (Cues Only): For a fast, modular workflow with Cues, simply make your adjustments and drag the modified Media or Surface directly on top of the existing Cue to overwrite it (or drop it into an empty slot to create a brand new one).

Right-Click Scene & Cue Options

Right-clicking directly on a Scene or Cue in the grid opens a context menu with several management options.

  • Update / Update Startup Values: Instantly overwrites the saved state with your current live output parameters.

  • Add To Timeline: Pushes the state directly into the Conductor. For Cues, you can choose to send all objects, or filter the push to only include specific elements like Video Surfaces, Lasers, Fixtures, or Medias.

  • Layout: Allows you to insert or delete rows and columns to organize your grid.

  • Add Control / Copy OSC Address: Quickly maps external triggers to fire the Scene/Cue.

Pro-Tip: Saving Your Changes If you make live adjustments to a Scene, remember to right-click it and select Update Scene to save your work.

You can do the exact same thing for Cues, but Cues also offer a faster workflow: simply make your adjustments and drag the modified Media or Surface directly over the existing Cue to overwrite it, or drop it into an empty slot to create a brand new one.

Scene & Cue Settings (Three Dots Menu)

 

Clicking the three dots on a Scene or Cue opens its dedicated settings panel to define how it fires and looks in your grid.

  • Launch & Transition: Define the Launch Quantize behavior to lock triggers to the beat, select the Transition Type (e.g., Linear), and set the fade Duration in seconds.

  • Representation: Customize the button's appearance in the grid by assigning a specific color code, uploading a custom image (or using the media thumbnail), and writing a text Comment for your operator.

Turning Cues into Timelines

With MadMapper 6, Scenes and Cues are no longer just static snapshots; they can become distinct Timelines themselves. This allows you to fine-tune transitions, precisely modify parameter changes down to the millisecond, and animate elements for live busking or pre-planned shows.

 

Accessing Edit Mode & Animating

To begin modifying or animating a cue, you must first enter Edit Mode. You can do this in two ways:

  1. Right-Click: Right-click an existing Scene or Cue in the grid and select Edit.

  2. The Edit Button: Alternatively, you can click the Edit button located at the bottom right of the main Scenes & Cues UI.

 

Once in Edit mode, the Scenes and Cues timeline will appear at the bottom of your screen, presenting the option to animate your parameters.

 

 

Clicking the Animate button instantly creates a timeline track within that specific cue where you can begin adding keyframes or recording new elements.

 

After clicking Animate, you will be given a timeline track for the specific scene or cue that you have selected. From here you can animate your sequences using the same timeline editing logic (see below).

 

The Scenes & Cues Inspector

To view and modify the deep settings of your cues and tracks, you need to utilize the Inspector panel.

  • Toggling the Inspector: When you select a track value or cue, you can toggle the inspector panel open using the Inspector button on the far right of the interface. 

(Note: You will simply see "No Selection" displayed in the panel if a track or cue is not actively selected). 

When you select an active Scene or Cue, the Inspector reveals how it interacts with the timeline:

  • Update from current values: A prominent button to quickly overwrite the stored state with your current live output.

  • Track Transition: When this cue is dropped into a master timeline, you can define how this clip transitions. You can choose No transition, Use timeline transition settings (inheriting global rules), or Use local transition settings (allowing you to set a custom duration in seconds just for this instance).

Timeline Navigation & UI Controls

Navigating complex timelines requires efficient zooming and track management. MadMapper provides several shortcuts and UI toggles to speed up your workflow:

  • Horizontal Zoom: Hold Option + Scroll Up/Down to shrink or expand the timeline horizontally.

  • Vertical Track Zoom: Hold Command + Scroll Up/Down to expand or collapse the tracks vertically.

  • Clip Navigation: Press Tab to jump the playhead to the next clip on the track. Press Shift + Tab to move to the previous clip.

Filters:

  • Track Pinning: Click the Pin icon to isolate and show only the tracks you have actively pinned. Pinned tracks will also show their respective keyframes.

  • Show Animated Tracks: Click the stopwatch icon to filter the view, showing only tracks that contain active keyframes.

  • Search: Use the search bar to instantly locate a specific element or parameter track by name.

Timeline Settings & Time Formatting

 

 

You can customize how time is calculated and displayed across your sequence. By clicking the Three Dots (...) icon in the upper timeline toolbar, you can access the core settings:

  • FPS → BPM Setup: Define the relationship between your frame rate and musical tempo by setting your target FPS, master BPM, and musical Signature (e.g., 4/4 time).

  • Playback Mode: Choose how the timeline advances. Options include Default (internal clock), Controlled Speed (variable playback rate), Global BPM (locked to the main MadMapper tempo), or External Sync (driven by incoming timecode).

  • Ruler Formatting: Change the visual increments on your timeline ruler by selecting the dropdown next to the time display. View your timeline in Seconds, exact Frames, or musical beat divisions ranging from 1/32 notes up to 4 bars (or turn it Off).

Timeline Right-Click Operations

 

Right-clicking directly on the timeline ruler or an empty track space provides high-level arrangement tools:

  • Display: A quick toggle to switch the ruler formatting between Time, Time/Frames, or Time/Bars.

  • Add Keyframes: Instantly drop keyframes across all tracks or only modified tracks at the playhead.

  • Insert / Delete Time: Opens a dialog to shift the entire arrangement forward or backward by a specific duration.

  • Loop Controls: Rapidly define the active loop bracket by selecting Set as Loop Start, Set as Loop End, or Move Loop here.

Creating & Editing Timelines (Keyframes)

A Scene or Cue that is converted into a timeline will include the track parameters within MadMapper that it has saved. So for example, if your Scene or Cue is assigned to a specific surface, FX, or UI element (Sliders, Buttons, Sources, etc.) then those stored settings will appear in the list of tracks.

 

 

To modify keyframes, add new tracks to the timeline, or add new parameters, simply click the Record button at the top of the timeline you are trying to edit. Then click on the UI element or slider that you'd like to change, and MadMapper will automatically create a keyframe for that element based on the values you've selected.  With Record button still active you can advance through the timeline and make new adjustments, and add new tracks and keyframes to your existing timeline.  When you are done, simply click the Record button again to disable it.

When a track has been created, you can double-click with your mouse on the keyframe line and it will create a new keyframe that you can modify further.

 

When a parameter track is armed, any changes you make in the interface will generate a keyframe. Right-clicking on any recorded keyframe opens the interpolation menu, allowing you to define the mathematical transition between points:

  • None (n): The value holds flat and instantly snaps to the next keyframe.

  • Linear (l): Creates a constant, straight-line transition between two values.

  • Bezier (b): Adds adjustable handles to manually draw ease-in and ease-out curves.

  • Auto Curve (c): The software automatically calculates a smooth, spline-based transition.

  • Additional Tools: This menu also allows you to Simplify (reduce unnecessary keyframes), Quantize (snap keyframes to the grid), or manually Move Playhead Here.

 

Track Types & Inspectors

 

Clicking the + (Plus) button on a timeline allows you to add specific tracks to control different data streams. Each track type has its own specific context menus and inspector settings.

Timeline Player Tracks

Timeline Player Tracks allow you to directly arrange existing Scenes and Cues in a linear timeline based format.

 

 

 

Note: The Scenes and Cues will use the fades assigned to them under the Scenes and Cues tab or their individual timelines.

 

To add an existing Scene or Cue to a Timeline Player Track.  Simply double-click on the empty track and a box will pop up where you can search and select your individual Scenes and Cues.

 

When a scene is in the timeline, right-click on the clip to see more options.

 

Right-Click Clip Options: You can quickly Split Clip, Quantize, Copy/Cut, or navigate the playhead and loop brackets directly to the clip.

Montage Tracks

Brings non-linear editing directly into MadMapper. You can arrange, cut, snap, and time-reverse standard video and image content directly on the timeline.

 

You can arrange multiple montage tracks and route them to different sources (Layers, Lasers, DMX, etc.).  When you place two montage tracks next to each other, MadMapper will add a cross fade indicator that can be adjusted for different fade durations.

 

 

After you have created your montage track, they are available in the Media Bin for you to assign to other surfaces like any other media clip.

 

  • Media Bin Right-Click: Right-clicking a montage item in the bin allows you to Select surfaces using this media or Add to Timeline.

 

  • Clip Right-Click: On the timeline itself, right-clicking a movie clip allows you to Split Clip, Preview, Replace Visual, or Select all clips with same Visual.

 

 

  • Montage Inspector: Here you can view a live Preview of the track. You can set the No Media Behavior (choosing whether empty track space causes the surface to Skip Rendering entirely, or render solid Black) and choose to Force Media Resolution.

 

 

Audio Tracks

Drop an audio file into the timeline for visual synchronization, FFT frequency analysis, or Automated BeatTracking to drive generative visuals.

 

When an audio track is in the timeline, you can use the show inspector icon in the right side of the timeline window to make visual adjustments and changes to the audio routing.

 

 

  • Right-Click on the Clip: Standard arrangement options including Split Clip and Quantize.

 

  • Audio Clip Inspector: Controls parameters specific to the selected segment of audio. You can view the precise Time, Duration, and Start Offset. You can also manage the File reference, adjust Clip Gain, change the Clip Speed percentage, and view the Detected BPM.

 

 

  • Audio Track Inspector: Controls global settings for the entire track. You can adjust the master Track Gain and toggle the visual Preview between a Spectrum analyzer or standard Waveform.

 

Examples of Waveform vs Spectrum:

 

  • Background & Routing: In the Track Inspector, you can toggle Use as Background to keep the audio running beneath other operations, and utilize the Audio Routing matrix to send specific track channels to specific hardware outputs.

OSC Tracks

Sequence network commands to drive external lighting consoles or show control systems.

 

  • Data Types: When creating the track, choose between Float, Integer, String, Color, Bool, or Events.

  • OSC Inspector: Define the target OSC Device, write the exact OSC Address (e.g., /int-1), and establish the Min Value and Max Value boundaries for the automation. (Note: Clicking the Three Dots will open the main project preferences for network routing).

MIDI Tracks

Send sequenced control data to external hardware or instruments.

 

  • Data Types: When creating the track, choose between standard Control Change (CC), High Resolution CC (14 bits), Pitch Bend, or specific musical Notes.

  • MIDI Inspector: Define the target MIDI Device and select the outbound Channel (1-16). (Note: Clicking the Three Dots will open the main project preferences for MIDI routing).

Time Markers & Follow Actions

Markers allow you to add interactive logic to an otherwise linear timeline, creating complex loops or wait states. This comes in handy for shows where you want to hold at a pause point until you hit GO / (play button) to continue playing forward in the timeline. You can add a Marker to the top ruler and customize it in the Inspector.

Note: You can tell what type of marker is in the timeline based on the Prefix. P/ = Pause, L/ = Loop, G/ = Go to Marker.

 

Click the Flag icon to add a marker:

 

  • Marker Display: Color-code your markers and choose to display their exact location in Seconds, Frames, or Time Code using the Three Dots menu.

 

  • Manual Actions: The Inspector provides Go and Go and Play buttons to manually jump the playhead to that specific marker during a live show.

 

  • Default Actions: Define what happens automatically when the playhead naturally hits the marker. Options include Pause (wait for user input), Continue (ignore the marker and keep playing), Loop (return to a previous point), or Go to Marker.

 

 

  • Follow Actions: Program secondary logic chains by adding a Follow Action (e.g., instructing the timeline to jump to "Marker 1", or to execute a sequence like Go to Marker and Pause or Start Timeline).

Clips    

Clips are short, repeatable segments created independently from the main sequence. If you bring a video file into your Montage for editing, cut an in and out point, then right-click > create clip. MadMapper will now treat this clip like it's an isolated 'clip'.

 

After creating and selecting the clip, you will then have options under the Media Inspector to Enable Looping, adjust the clip Speed, and Modify the In & Out points of the clip itself.

 

Exporting a Timeline from the Conductor

MadMapper allows you to render and export your fully programmed timeline directly from the Conductor. This feature lets you bounce your synchronized video, audio, laser, and DMX sequences into standard files for playback on other systems, archiving, or sharing with clients.

To access these settings, navigate to your Conductor timeline and select the export option.

Export Dialogue Box

At the very top of the Export Timeline dialog, you must choose how much of your timeline you want to render:

  • Export until last key frame / marker: Renders the timeline from the very beginning up to the exact moment of the final programmed event, keyframe, or marker.

  • Export Loop section: Renders only the specific segment of time defined by the active loop brackets on your Conductor timeline.

Export Video Outputs

This section allows you to render your mapped video outputs into standard video files or image sequences.

  • Target Outputs: Select exactly which video outputs you want to render by checking their respective boxes (e.g., Video-Output-1).

  • Include Audio: Check this box next to an output to embed the timeline's audio mix directly into that resulting video file.

  • Quality Slider: Use this to balance file size and visual fidelity when using compressed formats like H.264.

Available Video Codecs:

  • H264 & HEVC: Standard compressed formats, ideal for sharing previews or web delivery.

  • ProRes: High-quality Apple codec, excellent for visually lossless editing and playback on macOS.

  • Hap, HapAlpha, HapQ: GPU-accelerated formats optimized for multi-layer live performance software.

  • Image Sequence: Exports every single frame of your timeline as an individual image file.

Export Laser & DMX Outputs

If your project contains hardware control data, you can export these specialized streams alongside your video.

  • ILDA Export (Laser Data): Bounces your programmed laser paths and generative animations into a standard ILDA movie file.

    • Compatibility: Because it writes to the universal ILDA standard, this exported file is fully compatible with any third-party laser control software that supports ILDA movies (such as Pangolin BEYOND or standard show controllers).

    • High-Resolution Vectorization: As noted in the Expert Mode settings, utilizing the Timeline export allows you to bypass real-time hardware limitations, enabling you to process massive video vectorization tasks (up to 16k resolution) into flawless ILDA sequences.

  • DMX Export (Lighting Data): Renders your lighting programming into a highly optimized, proprietary MadLight Sequence. (Note: Because this is a proprietary format, these files cannot be imported into third-party lighting consoles).

    • Delta Compression: To keep file sizes highly efficient, the export stores all of your DMX universes together but only records the changes in value over time. The data is then automatically zipped.

    • File Chunking: The software automatically cuts the export into smaller file chunks. This prevents the export from hitting the standard 32-bit file size limits found on FAT32-formatted USB drives.

    • Optimized Scrubbing: By splitting the export into smaller files, the time-offset index is built directly into the filenames rather than requiring a massive index at the beginning of a single file. This allows for incredibly fast timeline scrubbing and ensures smooth, low-overhead playback on lightweight standalone hardware (such as Raspberry Pi-based media players).

Export Audio Output

If you need an isolated audio mixdown of your timeline, enable the audio export section.

  • Audio Codec: Choose between uncompressed WAV for the highest fidelity, or compressed MP3 and M4A formats for smaller file sizes.

  • Audio Channels: Select the number of audio channels (e.g., 2 for a standard stereo mix).

  • Sample Rate: Define the audio resolution (e.g., 44100 Hz for standard CD quality).

Setting the Destination

At the bottom of the window, verify the Export Folder path. This is the directory on your hard drive where all selected video, audio, ILDA, and DMX files will be generated once you click OK. You can use the browse button on the right to designate a specific folder, or click the reset icon to return to the default MadMapper workspace directory.
