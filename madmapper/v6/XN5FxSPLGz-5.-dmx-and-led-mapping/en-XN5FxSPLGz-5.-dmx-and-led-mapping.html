DMX & LED Mapping

MadMapper is not just a video tool; its integrated MadLight engine allows you to seamlessly bridge the gap between video pixels and physical lighting. Whether you are mapping a few RGB tubes, complex architectural moving heads, or a massive pixel-mapped stage design, the DMX features allow you to translate your visual compositions directly into physical light.

DMX Fundamentals

Before patching fixtures, it is important to understand how MadMapper communicates with your physical lighting hardware.

 

DMX Preferences: To route your lighting data, navigate to Preferences > Project (tab) > DMX. Here you can select your network interface, choose your USB DMX device, Art-Net or sACN, and define your framerate to ensure smooth playback across your rig.

 

  • DMX512: The standard lighting control protocol. A single DMX cable can carry 512 channels of data. This bundle of 512 channels is called a Universe. For example, a standard RGB LED pixel uses 3 channels (Red, Green, Blue). Therefore, one DMX universe can control up to 170 individual RGB pixels (170 x 3 = 510). If you are controlling lights that go beyond 512 channels, then they will stretch over onto a new universe. Some external hardware devices are limited to output over a single universe, while others can output over 96 universes through a single device.

  • Art-Net & sACN: Because large LED installations often require tens of thousands of channels, routing physical DMX cables for every universe is impossible. Art-Net and sACN are network protocols that send massive amounts of DMX universes over standard Ethernet cables. MadMapper outputs these protocols to network nodes (like PixLite, Advatek, or Enttec boxes), which then translate the data back into physical DMX or SPI signals for your lights.

Current hardware supported by MadMapper are the following USB DMX Devices: garageCube, ShowJockey, Enttec DMX USB Pro, Enttec DMX USB Pro MK2, and the DMX King eDMX2 Pro, and network based devices that support Art-Net or sACN.

 

Output Configuration: To route your lighting data, navigate to Preferences > Project (tab) > DMX. Here you can select your network interface, choose your USB DMX device, Art-Net or sACN, and define your framerate to ensure smooth playback across your rig.

DMX Filtering

DMX Filtering controls how MadMapper samples the video content for your lighting fixtures. Applying a filter averages the surrounding pixels, which helps prevent harsh flickering when playing high-contrast video over low-resolution LEDs.

  • None: Samples a single, exact pixel at the center of the fixture.
  • Box: Averages a square grid of pixels around the sample point (defined by the Size parameter).
  • Anamorphic: Averages an area with independent width and height parameters (X and Y), which is perfect for mapping rectangular LED strips or non-square pixels.

DMX Fixture Definitions

A DMX Fixture Definition describes the channel layout of a DMX lighting device so MadMapper knows how to control it. MadMapper ships with a built-in library of common fixtures (generic RGB pixels, RGBW strips, etc.), but you can create custom definitions for any DMX device — moving heads, laser fixtures, LED bars, fog machines, or complex multi-channel fixtures like the Minuit Une IVL Dice.

Fixture definitions are stored as .mmfl files (MadMapper Fixture Library). These files can be imported, exported, and shared between projects and users.

 

Opening the Editor

  1. Go to the DMX Fixtures tab (Cmd+4 / Ctrl+4)
  2. Click DMX + to create a new fixture
  3. In the fixture's properties, click Edit next to Fixture Library

This opens the Fixture Editor panel, which lists all available fixture definitions — both built-in and custom.

Keyboard shortcut to open the Fixture Editor panel directly: Option+Cmd+F (Mac) / Alt+Ctrl+F (Windows)

Creating a New Fixture

  1. In the Fixture Editor, click the + button
  2. Set the fixture name and group (used for organization in the library)
  3. Define the pixel grid dimensions (Width × Height) — use 1×1 for non-pixel fixtures
  4. Set the Pixel Type to Custom for non-standard channel layouts
  5. Add channels in order, specifying each channel's type, label, and bit depth

LED Array Configuration & Properties

When creating or editing a fixture in the Fixture Editor, the Properties panel provides the following options for arrays and matrices:

 

Size Mode

Option Description
Fixed Size Manually set the pixel grid dimensions (Width × Height)
LED Strip Mode Configure as a linear strip of pixels

Mapping Modes

  • Fixed Size: Manually set the pixel grid dimensions (Width × Height). In this mode, you can customize the individual pixel patch by clicking directly on any pixel in the Pixel Mapping interface to toggle it between its DMX address and UNPATCHED.

  • LED Strip Mode: Configure the fixture as a linear strip. Once selected, you can adjust the specific number of LED pixels directly in the Fixture Inspector panel.

  • Matrix Mode: Configure the fixture as a 2D pixel grid. Similar to LED Strip Mode, you can adjust the number of matrix divisions in the Fixture Inspector to match your physical hardware.

 

Fixture Properties & Mapping

The Properties panel allows you to define the geometric structure of your fixture, while the Pixel Mapping interface gives you granular control over individual pixel addressing.

 

Pixel Type — Select the color model for the fixture's pixels: RGB, RGBW, or Custom (for non-standard channel layouts like the IVL Dice).

Pixel Size — The number of DMX channels per pixel (e.g., 3 for RGB, 4 for RGBW). Check Advanced to access per-channel type and label configuration.

Assignation — Defines the pixel scanning/addressing order for LED strips and matrices. The 16 arrow pattern icons represent different ways pixels can be wired through the grid: left-to-right then top-to-bottom (Z-pattern), serpentine/zigzag, bottom-to-top, column-first, and their mirrored and reversed variants. Select the pattern that matches how your LEDs are physically wired. Click Clear to reset.

Skip channels 511–512 — When enabled, MadMapper will leave DMX channels 511 and 512 empty at the end of each universe. Some DMX devices reserve these channels, and enabling this avoids addressing conflicts at universe boundaries.

Channel Types

Each channel in a fixture definition has a type that tells MadMapper how to interpret it:

Type Description Typical Use
R Red RGB/RGBW color mixing
G Green RGB/RGBW color mixing
B Blue RGB/RGBW color mixing
W White RGBW fixtures
L Luminance Single-channel brightness
C Cyan CMY color mixing
M Magenta CMY color mixing
Y Yellow CMY color mixing
Slider Generic DMX value (0–255) Dimmer, pan, tilt, speed, gobo, or any non-color channel with manual control
Expression GLSL expression Computed channel value driven by a formula (see Expression Channels below)
Unused Empty/skipped channel Placeholder for reserved or unneeded channels in the fixture's DMX footprint

Bit Depth

Channels can be set to 8 Bits (single channel, 0–255) or 16 Bits (two channels, coarse + fine, 0–65535). 

 

Expression Channels

Expression channels let you compute a DMX value using a GLSL expression instead of a fixed slider or color input. This is useful for deriving channel values from other parameters — for example, calculating a checksum, inverting a value, or mixing multiple inputs mathematically.

 

The following variables are available inside an expression:

Variable Description
R, G, B Current red, green, and blue color values
L Luminance value
W White value
C, M, Y Cyan, magenta, and yellow values
INDEX The pixel index within the fixture
posX, posY Normalized pixel position within the fixture grid
COMP Component index
time Current time value
Slider names The values of any Slider channels, referenced by their label name

Expressions follow GLSL syntax, so standard math functions like sin(), clamp(), mix(), step(), etc. are available.

Controlling Moving Lights

MadMapper's fixture engine is not limited to LED strips; it is fully capable of driving intelligent fixtures, moving heads, and complex DMX devices (like fog machines or the Minuit Une IVL Dice), but the logic you are used to to control the devices will be slightly different from traditional DMX consoles.

Instead of relying on a traditional programmer with faders and palettes for every parameter, MadMapper drives the color and intensity of the fixture by sampling your video content, while generating custom UI sliders for the mechanical features.

To control moving lights:

  1. Create a custom fixture definition with a 1x1 pixel grid.
  2. Set the Pixel Type to Custom.
  3. Map the manufacturer's DMX sheet to the channels. Assign the color parameters to R, G, B types (so they sample the video map).
  4. Assign the Pan, Tilt, Strobe, and Gobo parameters to the Slider type.
  5. In your MadMapper workspace, these Sliders will now appear in the Fixture Inspector, allowing you to manually aim the moving heads while their colors dynamically react to your video content!

Tip: After you have created a slider, you can animate it with keyframes in the Timelines tab, or connect it to Oscillator modules created under the Modules tab to generate automatic, continuous movement.

Real-World Example: PAR 6-Channel

For standard pixel mapping with addressable RGB LED pixels, a fixture will not require a complex definition. But this example shows how MadMapper fixtures can be customized to work with non-standard fixtures like an LED PAR can with additional color diodes, such as Amber and UV (Ultraviolet).

This fixture is a 6-channel LED PAR. It uses a 1×1 pixel grid with the Pixel Type set to "Custom" and a Pixel Size of 6 channels. The first four channels are assigned to standard color types (R, G, B, and W) so they can directly sample the video map. The remaining two channels are dedicated to the Amber and UV diodes.

By setting these last two channels to the "Slider" type and labeling them "Amber" and "UV", MadMapper automatically generates dedicated sliders for them at the bottom of the Fixture Inspector. This allows the operator to manually mix in Amber and UV light independently while the main RGBW channels dynamically react to the video content.

  1. Open the Fixture Editor (click Edit next to Fixture Library on any fixture, or use Option+Cmd+F / Alt+Ctrl+F)
  2. Click Import...
  3. Select a .mmfl file
  4. The fixture appears in the library and is ready to assign

For large installations with many fixtures, MadMapper supports CSV import to batch-create fixtures with names, groups, and DMX addresses. Since MadMapper 5.2, the CSV can include group hierarchy using / notation (e.g., "Stage Fixtures/Fixture 1"). See File > Import DMX Fixtures from CSV for details.

For spatial layouts, MadMapper can import fixture positions from SVG files created in Illustrator or similar tools. This is especially useful for complex LED installations where fixture placement and DMX patching need to match a physical design precisely.

Tips for Custom Fixtures

Naming conventions — Use clear, descriptive channel labels. Include "Fine" in the label for the LSB channel of 16-bit pairs so operators can quickly identify coarse/fine relationships.

Modes — Many fixtures have multiple DMX modes with different channel counts. Create a separate definition for each mode and include the mode in the product name (e.g., "Minuit Une IVL Dice Mode 1").

Testing — After creating a fixture definition, use the DMX Monitor panel (Option+Cmd+M / Alt+Ctrl+M) to verify that channels are mapping correctly and values are reaching the expected DMX addresses.

Color channel types — Use the correct R/G/B/W/C/M/Y types for color channels rather than Slider. This allows MadMapper's color engine to properly drive them with pixel-mapped media, materials, and color pickers.

Cross-universe fixtures — If your fixture spans more than 512 channels or if its DMX address places it near the end of a universe, enable avoidCrossUniversePixels to prevent a single pixel's channels from being split across two universes.

Backup — Keep your custom .mmfl files in a shared location or version control. Fixture definitions are stored within MadMapper's library, but having external backups ensures you can quickly rebuild on a new machine.

LED Scanner

When building custom LED installations, placing physical light tubes or pixel tape rarely results in a perfect geometric grid. Trying to manually arrange dozens of virtual DMX fixtures in MadMapper to match their scattered real-world positions is notoriously difficult. The LED Scanner automates this entirely.

Setup & Configuration

  1. Position the Camera: Position a camera (webcam or DSLR) so it has a clear view of your entire physical LED installation.

  2. Open the Scanner: Access the LED Scanner via Tools > Scanners > LED Scanner.

  3. Configure Parameters: In the LED Scanner panel, define the following settings:

    • Camera: Select the input device (e.g., FaceTime HD Camera).

    • Fixture Definition: Choose the specific profile (e.g., GarageCube - SFX-01 RGB 1m).

    • DMX Range: Define the Start Universe/Channel and End Universe/Channel that the scanner should monitor for your LED installation.

    • Brightness & Threshold: Adjust these sliders to ensure the camera can clearly distinguish the flashing LEDs from the ambient light in the room.

    • Map LEDs at 0,0 if not detected: An optional safety check to catch any pixels that were missed during the scan.

  4. The Scan: Click Start Scan. MadMapper will sequentially flash every single LED pixel in your physical installation one by one.

  5. The Result: The camera tracks these flashes in real-time. Once finished, MadMapper automatically arranges all of your virtual DMX fixtures in your MadLight workspace to perfectly mirror their exact physical coordinates in the real world. You can immediately drop a video texture across your workspace, and it will map flawlessly across your physical LEDs.

(See the Special Tools and Auto-Calibration page for detailed instructions on using the LED, Space, or Laser Scanners.

MadLight Recorder

Sometimes you need to capture a complex, generative lighting look and bake it down into a static file for playback on a standalone architectural controller, for looping inside a sequence, or for export to MiniMad hardware.

The MadLight Recorder allows you to record the live, outgoing Art-Net or sACN streams directly from MadMapper. It captures the exact DMX values frame-by-frame and saves them into a highly optimized MadLight Sequence. This allows you to effortlessly record a live busking session or a complex generative pixel map, and play it back later, export it to the Timeline, or prepare it for deployment on dedicated hardware.

 

Audio & Synchronization

When an audio file is associated with a sequence, you can toggle the Sync setting to control playback behavior:

  • Sync Enabled (Checked): The audio track will stay in perfect synchronization with the lighting track.

  • Sync Disabled (Unchecked): The audio will loop independently from the lighting playback.

Exporting to MiniMad

Once a sequence is recorded, you can use the Export to MiniMad button to bundle your sequence and audio files for use on MiniMad players. This provides a streamlined workflow for moving from complex generative setups in MadMapper to robust, standalone playback in the field.
